Black Wattle Tree
The Black Wattle Tree (Acacia mearnsii), also called Australian Black Wattle, is a fast-growing hardwood tree native to Australia. It is valued for its tannin-rich bark, durable timber, and bright yellow flowers.

Black Wattle trees grow 10–25 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. They have bipinnately compound leaves and produce clusters of fragrant, bright yellow flowers. The tree thrives in full sun and well-drained soils and is moderately drought-tolerant.

Characteristics of Black Wattle Trees

  • Bipinnately compound leaves
  • Clusters of bright yellow, fragrant flowers
  • Moderately drought-tolerant and adaptable to various soils
  • Straight trunk with rounded crown
  • Fast-growing, medium-sized tree
  • Native to Australia

Common Uses

  • Source of tannins for leather processing
  • Timber for furniture and construction
  • Ornamental planting in gardens and parks
  • Soil stabilization and erosion control
  • Habitat for wildlife, including birds and insects
