Cacao Tree
Cacao trees are tropical evergreen trees of the genus Theobroma. They are native to the deep tropical regions of Central and South America and are cultivated globally for their seeds, which are the source of chocolate and cocoa products.

Cacao trees grow in warm, humid rainforest environments with shaded understory conditions. They produce pods directly from the trunk and larger branches, each containing cocoa beans surrounded by sweet pulp.

Characteristics of Cacao Trees

  • Evergreen tropical understory trees
  • Produce pods directly from trunk and branches (cauliflory)
  • Require warm, humid, shaded environments
  • Broad glossy leaves adapted for rainforest conditions
  • Seeds (cocoa beans) are the raw source of chocolate

Common Uses

  • Chocolate production from cocoa beans
  • Cocoa powder and cocoa butter extraction
  • Beverages and confectionery products
  • Agroforestry systems in tropical regions
  • Support for rainforest biodiversity and shade farming systems
