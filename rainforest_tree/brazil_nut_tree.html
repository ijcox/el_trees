Brazil Nut Tree
The Brazil nut tree (Bertholletia excelsa) is a large evergreen tree native to the Amazon rainforest of South America. It is renowned for producing Brazil nuts, an important edible seed with high nutritional and economic value.

Brazil nut trees thrive in humid tropical rainforest climates with well-drained, deep soils. They are typically found in wild forests but are also cultivated in agroforestry systems for nut production.

Characteristics of Brazil Nut Trees

  • Evergreen trees with large, straight trunks
  • Can grow up to 160 feet tall in ideal rainforest conditions
  • Produce large, woody fruits containing 10–25 seeds (nuts)
  • Long-lived trees, often exceeding 500 years
  • Depend on specific pollinators like orchid bees for reproduction

Common Uses

  • Consumption of raw, roasted, or processed Brazil nuts
  • Oil extracted from nuts used in cooking and cosmetics
  • Agroforestry and sustainable rainforest nut harvesting
  • Timber occasionally used for construction or furniture
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological importance in the Amazon
