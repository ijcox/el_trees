Plane Tree
Plane trees (Platanus spp.), also known as sycamores in some regions, are large deciduous trees native to North America, Europe, and Asia. They are recognized for their distinctive peeling bark, broad leaves, and adaptability to urban environments.

Plane trees grow best in full sun with well-drained soil and are highly tolerant of pollution and compacted urban soils. They are often planted in avenues, parks, and large gardens for shade and ornamental appeal.

Characteristics of Plane Trees

  • Large deciduous trees, 50–100 feet tall with wide, spreading canopy
  • Leaves are broad, lobed, and resemble maple leaves
  • Bark is mottled and peels away in plates, revealing lighter wood beneath
  • Flowers are inconspicuous, appearing in spring; fruits are small spherical clusters
  • Fast-growing, long-lived, and highly tolerant of urban conditions

Common Uses

  • Shade tree in parks, streets, and large gardens
  • Ornamental planting for aesthetic appeal and bark patterns
  • Urban landscaping due to pollution tolerance
  • Timber occasionally used for furniture, veneer, and small woodworking projects
  • Provides habitat for urban wildlife, including birds and insects
