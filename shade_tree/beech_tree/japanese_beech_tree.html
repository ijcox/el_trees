Japanese Beech Tree
The Japanese Beech Tree (Fagus crenata) is a deciduous tree known for its smooth bark and dense, rounded canopy. It is native to Japan and plays an important role in mountain forest ecosystems.

Japanese Beech trees typically grow 20–30 meters tall with a broad crown and sturdy trunk. They thrive in cool, humid climates and are commonly found in mountainous regions. Their forests are known for rich biodiversity and seasonal beauty.

Characteristics of Japanese Beech Trees

  • Smooth gray bark
  • Dense, rounded canopy
  • Leaves are oval with slightly serrated edges
  • Produces small beechnuts
  • Prefers cool, humid environments
  • Native to Japan

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in parks and gardens
  • Timber for furniture and construction
  • Forest conservation and habitat support
  • Wildlife food source
  • Reforestation in mountainous regions
