Linden Tree
Linden trees (Tilia spp.), also known as basswood or lime trees, are large deciduous trees native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are valued for their dense foliage, fragrant flowers, and use in landscaping and timber production.

Linden trees prefer full sun to partial shade and well-drained, fertile soil. They are commonly planted in parks, streets, and large gardens for shade, ornamental appeal, and ecological benefits.

Characteristics of Linden Trees

  • Large deciduous trees, typically 50–80 feet tall with broad, dense canopy
  • Leaves are heart-shaped, serrated, and bright green turning yellow in autumn
  • Fragrant, small, pale yellow flowers bloom in late spring to early summer
  • Fruit is a small, nut-like drupe attached to a leafy bract
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived, tolerant of urban environments

Common Uses

  • Shade and ornamental tree in parks, streets, and gardens
  • Timber used for carving, furniture, and musical instruments
  • Flowers used for herbal teas and traditional medicine
  • Supports pollinators such as bees and butterflies
  • Urban landscaping due to pollution tolerance and low maintenance
