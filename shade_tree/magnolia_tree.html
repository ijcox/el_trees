Magnolia Tree
Magnolia trees are flowering trees of the genus Magnolia. They are native to East and Southeast Asia, as well as the Americas, and are admired for their large, fragrant flowers and glossy leaves.

Magnolias thrive in temperate to subtropical climates with well-drained, fertile soils. They are widely used as ornamental trees in gardens, parks, and streetscapes for their beauty and fragrance.

Characteristics of Magnolia Trees

  • Deciduous or evergreen trees with large, glossy leaves
  • Produce large, fragrant, showy flowers in spring or summer
  • Moderate growth rate, can reach 60–80 feet tall
  • Adapted to temperate and subtropical climates
  • Provide shade, ornamental beauty, and habitat for wildlife

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and streets
  • Shade trees for landscapes and residential areas
  • Timber from some species for furniture and woodwork
  • Wildlife habitat for birds, insects, and pollinators
  • Fragrant flowers used in perfumes and floral arrangements
