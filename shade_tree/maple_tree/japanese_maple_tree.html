Japanese Maple Tree
Japanese maple trees (Acer palmatum) are deciduous trees native to Japan, Korea, and China. They are prized for their delicate, deeply lobed leaves and stunning autumn colors, making them popular ornamental trees worldwide.

Japanese maple trees prefer temperate climates with well-drained soil, partial shade, and protection from strong winds. They are often planted in gardens, bonsai collections, and landscape designs for their visual appeal.

Characteristics of Japanese Maple Trees

  • Deciduous trees with deeply lobed, delicate leaves
  • Height ranges from 6–25 feet depending on variety
  • Leaves turn vibrant red, orange, or yellow in autumn
  • Slow-growing with a rounded or spreading canopy
  • Highly valued for bonsai and ornamental landscaping

Common Uses

  • Ornamental landscaping in gardens and yards
  • Bonsai cultivation for aesthetic appeal
  • Shade and visual interest in small garden spaces
  • Specimen trees for patios, parks, and courtyards
  • Autumn foliage display and decorative purposes
