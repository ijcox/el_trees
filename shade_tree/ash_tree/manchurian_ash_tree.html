Manchurian Ash Tree
The Manchurian Ash Tree (Fraxinus mandshurica) is a deciduous tree valued for its fast growth, tall stature, and strong wood. It is native to northeastern China, Korea, and eastern Russia and thrives in temperate climates.

Manchurian Ash trees typically grow 20–35 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They prefer moist, fertile soils and are often planted for timber production and reforestation. The leaves are compound with 7–9 leaflets, and the tree produces winged seeds called samaras.

Characteristics of Manchurian Ash Trees

  • Compound leaves with 7–9 leaflets
  • Tall, straight trunk with broad, rounded crown
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Hard, durable wood
  • Native to northeastern China, Korea, and eastern Russia

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and tools
  • Shade tree in parks and streets
  • Reforestation and forest conservation
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Ornamental planting in temperate landscapes
