Chinese Elm Tree
The Chinese Elm Tree (Ulmus parvifolia) is a deciduous to semi-evergreen tree native to East Asia. It is widely valued for its graceful form, mottled bark, and resistance to Dutch elm disease.

Chinese Elm trees grow 10–20 meters tall with a rounded, spreading crown. They adapt well to a variety of soils and tolerate urban conditions. The leaves are small, glossy, and serrated, and the tree produces winged seeds (samaras).

Characteristics of Chinese Elm Trees

  • Small, glossy, serrated leaves
  • Rounded, spreading crown
  • Distinctive mottled, exfoliating bark
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Highly resistant to Dutch elm disease
  • Native to East Asia

Common Uses

  • Ornamental tree for gardens, parks, and streets
  • Shade tree in urban environments
  • Bonsai cultivation
  • Soil stabilization and erosion control
  • Wildlife habitat
