American Elm Tree
The American Elm Tree (Ulmus americana) is a large deciduous tree native to North America. It is known for its graceful, vase-shaped canopy and was once widely planted along streets and in parks.

American Elm trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a broad, arching crown. They thrive in moist, well-drained soils and full sunlight. The leaves are oval with serrated edges, and the tree produces small winged seeds called samaras.

Characteristics of American Elm Trees

  • Oval, serrated leaves
  • Distinctive vase-shaped canopy
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Sensitive to Dutch elm disease
  • Native to North America

Common Uses

  • Shade tree for streets and parks
  • Ornamental planting in landscapes
  • Timber for furniture and veneer
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Urban greening and restoration projects
