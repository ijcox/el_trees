Siberian Elm Tree
The Siberian Elm Tree (Ulmus pumila) is a hardy deciduous tree native to Asia. It is known for its rapid growth and tolerance to drought and harsh conditions.

Siberian Elm trees grow 15–25 meters tall with a rounded, somewhat irregular crown. They thrive in a wide range of soils and tolerate drought, wind, and cold climates. The leaves are small, serrated, and the tree produces winged seeds (samaras).

Characteristics of Siberian Elm Trees

  • Small, serrated leaves
  • Rounded, irregular crown
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Highly drought- and cold-tolerant
  • Fast-growing and adaptable
  • Native to Asia

Common Uses

  • Windbreaks and shelterbelts
  • Shade tree in urban and rural areas
  • Soil stabilization and erosion control
  • Ornamental planting in tough environments
  • Wildlife habitat
