European White Elm Tree
The European White Elm Tree (Ulmus laevis) is a deciduous tree native to Europe. It is known for its broad crown, graceful branches, and tolerance to moist soils.

European White Elm trees grow 20–35 meters tall with a wide, spreading crown. They thrive in moist, fertile soils and are often found near rivers and floodplains. The leaves are oval and serrated, and the tree produces winged seeds (samaras).

Characteristics of European White Elm Trees

  • Oval, serrated leaves
  • Wide, spreading crown
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Tolerant of moist and floodplain soils
  • Moderate growth rate and long lifespan
  • Native to Europe

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Shade tree in natural and urban settings
  • Restoration of riverbanks and floodplains
  • Timber for light construction and furniture
  • Wildlife habitat
