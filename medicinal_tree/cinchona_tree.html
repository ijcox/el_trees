Cinchona Tree
The Cinchona Tree (Cinchona spp.) is a tropical evergreen tree native to the Andean forests of South America. It is famous for its bark, which contains quinine, historically used to treat malaria.

Cinchona trees grow best in subtropical to tropical climates with well-drained soil and moderate rainfall. They are cultivated in plantations for medicinal bark extraction and occasionally planted for ornamental and conservation purposes.

Common Types of Cinchona Trees

Cinchona Officinalis Cinchona Pubescens Cinchona Ledgeriana Cinchona Calisaya Cinchona Succirubra

Characteristics of Cinchona Trees

  • Evergreen trees, usually 20–50 feet tall
  • Leaves are glossy, elliptic, and opposite
  • Flowers are small, tubular, and range from pink to white, blooming in clusters
  • Bark is rough, corky, and contains alkaloids including quinine
  • Moderate growth rate; prefers high-altitude tropical forests

Common Uses

  • Medicinal: source of quinine for malaria treatment
  • Pharmaceutical: bark extracts for antimalarial and tonic products
  • Ornamental planting in tropical botanical gardens
  • Research on alkaloids and natural compounds
  • Conservation and reforestation in native habitats
