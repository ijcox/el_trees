Tea Tree
The Tea Tree (Melaleuca alternifolia) is a small evergreen tree native to Australia, renowned for its essential oil, which has antibacterial, antifungal, and anti-inflammatory properties.

Tea trees grow best in sunny, well-drained, sandy soils and can tolerate seasonal flooding. They are cultivated for essential oil production, medicinal uses, and occasionally ornamental purposes.

Characteristics of Tea Trees

  • Small to medium-sized evergreen tree, 10–25 feet tall
  • Leaves are narrow, aromatic, and alternately arranged
  • Flowers are small, white or cream-colored, appearing in fluffy spikes
  • Bark is papery and peeling, often white to brownish
  • Moderate growth rate; hardy in native climate and drought-tolerant once established

Common Uses

  • Essential oil extraction for antibacterial, antifungal, and antiseptic products
  • Traditional medicine and skin treatments
  • Ornamental planting for small gardens and landscaping
  • Environmental restoration and erosion control in native regions
  • Research on medicinal and cosmetic applications of Melaleuca species
