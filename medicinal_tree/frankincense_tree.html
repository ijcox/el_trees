Frankincense Tree
The Frankincense Tree (Boswellia spp.) is a small to medium-sized tree native to the arid regions of Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. It is famous for producing aromatic resin, known as frankincense, used for incense, perfumes, and traditional medicine.

Frankincense trees grow in dry, rocky, and well-drained soils, often on hillsides or semi-desert regions. They are slow-growing, drought-tolerant, and often harvested for their resin through careful tapping of the bark.

Common Types of Frankincense Trees

Boswellia Sacra Boswellia Carteri Boswellia Frereana Boswellia Papyrifera Boswellia Rivae

Characteristics of Frankincense Trees

  • Small to medium-sized trees, 10–30 feet tall
  • Leaves are pinnate, green, and grow in clusters
  • Flowers are small, white to pink, appearing in clusters
  • Bark is pale, smooth, and tapped to extract aromatic resin
  • Slow-growing and highly drought-tolerant, adapted to arid climates

Common Uses

  • Resin harvested for incense, perfumes, and religious ceremonies
  • Traditional medicine: anti-inflammatory and digestive uses
  • Ornamental planting in arid landscapes and gardens
  • Essential oils derived from resin for aromatherapy and skincare
  • Cultural and historical significance in trade and religious practices
