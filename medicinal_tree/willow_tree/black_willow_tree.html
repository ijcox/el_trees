Black Willow Tree
The Black Willow Tree (Salix nigra) is a deciduous tree native to North America. It is commonly found along rivers and wetlands and is known for its flexible branches and rapid growth.

Black Willow trees grow 10–20 meters tall with a narrow, irregular crown. They thrive in wet soils and are often found along stream banks and floodplains. The leaves are narrow and finely serrated, and the tree produces catkins in spring.

Characteristics of Black Willow Trees

  • Narrow, finely serrated leaves
  • Narrow, irregular crown
  • Produces catkins in spring
  • Highly tolerant of wet soils and flooding
  • Fast-growing and relatively short-lived
  • Native to North America

Common Uses

  • Soil stabilization along riverbanks and wetlands
  • Ornamental planting near water
  • Wood for crates, furniture, and pulp
  • Habitat for wildlife and pollinators
  • Restoration of riparian ecosystems
