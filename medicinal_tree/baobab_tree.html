Baobab Tree
The Baobab Tree (Adansonia spp.) is a large, iconic tree native to Africa, Madagascar, and Australia. It is known for its massive trunk, longevity, drought resistance, and nutritious fruits, often called the “Tree of Life.”

Baobabs thrive in arid to semi-arid climates with well-drained soils and full sunlight. They are often found in savannas, dry forests, and occasionally cultivated for their fruit and ornamental value.

Characteristics of Baobab Trees

  • Massive, swollen trunk storing water; can reach diameters over 30 feet
  • Height typically 50–100 feet
  • Leaves are palmate with multiple leaflets, deciduous in dry season
  • Flowers are large, white, and pollinated by bats
  • Fruit is an oblong, hard-shelled capsule rich in vitamin C and nutrients
  • Extremely long-lived; some specimens over 2,000 years old

Common Uses

  • Edible fruit pulp and seeds, used for nutrition and beverages
  • Traditional medicine from bark, leaves, and seeds
  • Ornamental and cultural symbol in Africa and Madagascar
  • Timber and fiber from bark for ropes and mats
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological benefits in dry ecosystems
