Boswellia Papyrifera
Boswellia papyrifera is a major source of commercial frankincense, widely harvested in Africa for its aromatic resin.

Boswellia papyrifera is native to northeastern Africa, particularly Ethiopia, Sudan, and Eritrea. It is one of the most extensively harvested species for frankincense production due to its abundance and resin yield.

The resin from this tree has been traded for centuries and is commonly used in incense, traditional medicine, and cultural practices.

Characteristics of Boswellia Papyrifera

  • Medium-sized deciduous tree
  • Grows in dry woodland and savanna regions
  • Produces large quantities of resin
  • Peeling bark with papery texture
  • Highly drought-tolerant
  • Important economic species

Common Uses

  • Commercial frankincense production
  • Incense and religious rituals
  • Traditional medicine
  • Export trade
  • Aromatherapy
