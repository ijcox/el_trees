Rhizophora Tree
The Rhizophora Tree, commonly referred to as the Red Mangrove (Rhizophora spp.), is a tropical mangrove species known for its distinctive prop roots that stabilize shorelines and provide critical habitats for marine life.

Rhizophora trees thrive in intertidal zones along tropical and subtropical coastlines. They are foundational species in mangrove ecosystems, protecting against erosion, supporting biodiversity, and improving water quality by trapping sediments.

Common Types of Rhizophora Trees

Rhizophora Mangle (Red Mangrove) Rhizophora Apiculata Rhizophora Mucronata Rhizophora Stylosa Rhizophora Hybrid

Characteristics of Rhizophora Trees

  • Medium to large mangrove trees, 30–70 feet tall
  • Distinctive stilt or prop roots for stability in tidal zones
  • Leaves are thick, dark green, glossy, and salt-tolerant
  • Flowers are small, white to pink; fruit is viviparous (seedlings germinate on the tree)
  • Thrives in saline, waterlogged environments
  • Forms dense coastal forests that support diverse marine and bird life

Common Uses

  • Shoreline stabilization and erosion control
  • Habitat for fish, crustaceans, and birds
  • Wood used locally for fuel, construction, and tools
  • Ecological restoration and mangrove conservation projects
  • Traditional medicine and cultural significance in coastal communities
