Black Mangrove Tree
The Black Mangrove Tree (Avicennia germinans) is a salt-tolerant coastal tree native to tropical and subtropical regions. It is known for its pneumatophores—specialized aerial roots that allow it to breathe in waterlogged soils.

Black Mangroves thrive in saline or brackish water along tidal shorelines and estuaries. They are often found behind Red Mangrove zones and are important for shoreline stabilization, wildlife habitat, and nutrient cycling in mangrove ecosystems.

Characteristics of Black Mangrove Trees

  • Medium-sized tree, 20–50 feet tall
  • Pneumatophores (aerial roots) for oxygen intake in waterlogged soil
  • Leaves are dark green, leathery, often with salt-excreting glands
  • Flowers are small, yellow, and clustered
  • Thrives in saline or brackish environments
  • Often forms dense thickets along shorelines

Common Uses

  • Coastal protection against erosion and storm surges
  • Provides habitat for fish, crustaceans, and birds
  • Wood used locally for fuel, poles, and crafts
  • Traditional medicine in some coastal communities
  • Ecological restoration and mangrove conservation
