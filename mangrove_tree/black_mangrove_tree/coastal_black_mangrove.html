Coastal Black Mangrove
A shoreline-adapted Black Mangrove that thrives in coastal wetlands and plays a vital role in erosion control.

The Coastal Black Mangrove is specially adapted to grow along shorelines and intertidal zones, where it helps stabilize sediments and reduce erosion. It is commonly found in salt marshes, estuaries, and coastal lagoons.

Its pneumatophore root system allows it to survive in waterlogged, oxygen-poor soils, while also providing habitat for marine organisms and birds.

Characteristics of Black Mangrove Trees

  • Pneumatophores for oxygen exchange
  • Highly salt-tolerant species
  • Thrives in coastal and tidal wetlands
  • Evergreen foliage
  • Stabilizes shoreline sediments
  • Supports coastal biodiversity

Common Uses

  • Coastal erosion prevention
  • Habitat for marine and bird species
  • Wetland restoration
  • Shoreline protection projects
  • Environmental conservation efforts
