Grey Mangrove Tree
The Grey Mangrove Tree (Avicennia marina) is a salt-tolerant coastal tree native to the Indo-Pacific region. It is adapted to intertidal zones and is known for its specialized aerial roots (pneumatophores) that allow respiration in waterlogged, saline soils.

Grey Mangroves thrive in brackish and saline waters, often forming dense stands along estuaries, lagoons, and tidal flats. They play a crucial role in coastal protection, sediment stabilization, and providing habitat for a wide variety of marine and terrestrial species.

Characteristics of Grey Mangrove Trees

  • Medium-sized tree, 20–50 feet tall
  • Pneumatophores (aerial roots) for oxygen intake in waterlogged soils
  • Leaves are grey-green, thick, and salt-excreting
  • Flowers are small, yellowish, clustered
  • Tolerates high salinity and periodic tidal flooding
  • Forms dense coastal forests supporting rich biodiversity

Common Uses

  • Coastal protection against erosion, tides, and storms
  • Habitat for fish, crustaceans, birds, and other wildlife
  • Wood used for fuel, construction, and small crafts
  • Ecological restoration and mangrove conservation projects
  • Traditional medicine in some coastal communities
