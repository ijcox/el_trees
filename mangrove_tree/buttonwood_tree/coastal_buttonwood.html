Coastal Buttonwood
A shoreline-adapted Buttonwood form that thrives in salty, windy coastal environments.

The Coastal Buttonwood is a naturally occurring form of Conocarpus Erectus found along shorelines, estuaries, and coastal flats. It is highly tolerant of salt spray and strong winds, making it a key species for stabilizing coastal environments.

This form often develops dense branching structures that help reduce erosion and provide shelter for coastal wildlife.

Characteristics of Buttonwood Trees

  • Evergreen coastal species
  • Strong wind and salt tolerance
  • Dense branching growth habit
  • Thrives in sandy and rocky soils
  • Excellent erosion resistance
  • Adapted to tropical and subtropical coasts

Common Uses

  • Coastal erosion control
  • Shoreline stabilization
  • Windbreak planting
  • Habitat support for wildlife
  • Coastal restoration projects
