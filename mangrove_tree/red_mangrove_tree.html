Red Mangrove Tree
The Red Mangrove Tree (Rhizophora mangle) is a coastal tree native to tropical and subtropical shorelines. It is known for its distinctive prop roots that stabilize coastlines and provide habitat for marine life.

Red Mangroves thrive in saline, waterlogged soils along tropical coasts. They are often found in estuaries, lagoons, and tidal rivers, forming dense mangrove forests that protect shorelines from erosion and storms.

Characteristics of Red Mangrove Trees

  • Medium-sized tree, 20–70 feet tall
  • Prop roots extending from trunk into water or soil for support
  • Thick, glossy, dark green leaves
  • Flowers small, white to pink; produce propagules (seedlings)
  • Salt-tolerant and adapted to waterlogged coastal environments
  • Forms dense forests providing ecological habitat

Common Uses

  • Coastal protection against erosion, storms, and waves
  • Habitat for fish, crustaceans, and birds
  • Traditional medicine from bark and leaves in some cultures
  • Wood used for fuel, construction, and crafts
  • Ecological restoration and mangrove conservation projects
