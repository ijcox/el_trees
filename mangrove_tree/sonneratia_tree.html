Sonneratia Tree
The Sonneratia Tree (Sonneratia spp.) is a genus of mangrove trees native to tropical and subtropical coastal regions of Asia, Africa, and the Pacific. These trees are recognized for their distinctive stilt roots and role in protecting shorelines and supporting coastal ecosystems.

Sonneratia trees thrive in tidal, saline environments such as estuaries, lagoons, and river mouths. They are key species in mangrove forests, providing stabilization of sediments, habitat for fish and birds, and protection against coastal erosion.

Common Types of Sonneratia Trees

Sonneratia Alba (Mangrove Apple) Sonneratia Caseolaris Sonneratia Apetala Sonneratia Ovata Sonneratia Acida

Characteristics of Sonneratia Trees

  • Medium to large mangrove trees, 30–70 feet tall
  • Distinctive stilt or buttress roots for stability in soft, waterlogged soils
  • Leaves are thick, glossy, and adapted to saline environments
  • Flowers are large, showy, and often fragrant; fruit is edible in some species
  • Highly salt- and water-tolerant
  • Provides habitat and food for fish, birds, and other wildlife

Common Uses

  • Coastal protection against erosion and storms
  • Supports fisheries and mangrove biodiversity
  • Wood used locally for fuel, construction, and small tools
  • Traditional medicine and food (fruit) in some cultures
  • Mangrove reforestation and ecological restoration projects
