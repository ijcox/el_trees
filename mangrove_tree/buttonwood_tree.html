Buttonwood Tree
The Buttonwood Tree (Conocarpus erectus) is a mangrove-like coastal tree native to tropical and subtropical regions. It is commonly found in brackish water areas and along coastal shorelines, providing ecological benefits such as erosion control and wildlife habitat.

Buttonwood trees thrive in full sun and well-drained, saline soils. They are often planted in coastal landscapes, urban areas, and mangrove restoration projects due to their hardiness and tolerance to saltwater.

Characteristics of Buttonwood Trees

  • Small to medium-sized tree, 20–35 feet tall
  • Leaves are thick, glossy, and green or silvery
  • Trunk often twisted with a dense canopy
  • Flowers are small, inconspicuous, and clustered
  • Thrives in saline and brackish environments
  • Provides ecological habitat for birds and coastal wildlife

Common Uses

  • Shoreline stabilization and erosion control
  • Ornamental planting in parks, gardens, and urban landscapes
  • Habitat for birds, fish, and other coastal wildlife
  • Wood used for small crafts or fuel in local communities
  • Ecological restoration in mangrove and coastal ecosystems
