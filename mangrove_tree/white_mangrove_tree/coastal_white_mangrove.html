Coastal White Mangrove
A shoreline-adapted White Mangrove form that thrives along coasts and plays a key role in erosion control and wetland stability.

The Coastal White Mangrove grows in intertidal zones where freshwater and seawater mix, often forming dense stands along shorelines. It is especially important in stabilizing sandy and muddy coasts, reducing the impact of waves and storm surge.

This mangrove type contributes to the formation of productive coastal ecosystems by trapping sediment and creating protected habitats for fish, birds, and invertebrates.

Characteristics of White Mangrove Trees

  • Moderate salt tolerance with coastal specialization
  • Thrives in tidal and shoreline environments
  • Evergreen coastal species
  • Helps stabilize sand and mud substrates
  • Supports diverse wetland ecosystems
  • Contributes to natural coastal protection

Common Uses

  • Coastal erosion prevention
  • Shoreline stabilization projects
  • Wetland restoration and conservation
  • Wildlife habitat support
  • Environmental protection efforts
