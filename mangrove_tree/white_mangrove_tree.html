White Mangrove Tree
The White Mangrove Tree (Laguncularia racemosa) is a coastal tree native to tropical and subtropical regions. It is commonly found in higher intertidal zones of mangrove forests and is valued for its role in shoreline protection and ecological habitat.

White Mangroves thrive in brackish or saline soils with moderate tidal flooding. They often grow behind Red and Black Mangroves and help stabilize shorelines while providing habitat for a variety of wildlife.

Characteristics of White Mangrove Trees

  • Small to medium-sized tree, 15–40 feet tall
  • Leaves are thick, oval, and green, often with salt-excreting glands
  • Roots are shallow, with some aerial roots in wetter areas
  • Flowers are small, cream-colored, clustered
  • Thrives in brackish, waterlogged soils with moderate tidal exposure
  • Forms dense forests providing shelter for wildlife

Common Uses

  • Shoreline stabilization and protection against erosion
  • Provides habitat for fish, crustaceans, and birds
  • Wood used locally for fuel and small crafts
  • Ecological restoration and mangrove conservation projects
  • Traditional medicine in some coastal communities
