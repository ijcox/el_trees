Avicennia Marina Tree
The Avicennia Marina Tree, commonly known as the Grey Mangrove, is a salt-tolerant mangrove species native to the Indo-Pacific region. It is well-adapted to intertidal zones and is distinguished by its aerial roots called pneumatophores, which allow it to breathe in waterlogged, saline soils.

Avicennia Marina thrives in brackish and saline environments, such as estuaries, lagoons, and tidal flats. It is a key species for coastal stabilization, erosion control, and providing habitat for marine and terrestrial wildlife.

Characteristics of Avicennia Marina Trees

  • Medium-sized tree, 20–50 feet tall
  • Pneumatophores (aerial roots) for oxygen intake in waterlogged soils
  • Leaves are grey-green, thick, and salt-excreting
  • Flowers are small, yellowish, and clustered
  • Tolerates high salinity, tidal flooding, and harsh coastal conditions
  • Forms dense mangrove forests supporting biodiversity

Common Uses

  • Coastal protection against erosion, waves, and storm surges
  • Provides habitat for fish, crustaceans, birds, and other wildlife
  • Wood used for fuel, poles, and small crafts
  • Ecological restoration and mangrove conservation projects
  • Traditional medicine in some coastal communities
