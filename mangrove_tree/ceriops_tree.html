Ceriops Tree
The Ceriops Tree (Ceriops spp.) is a genus of mangrove trees found in tropical and subtropical coastal regions of the Indo-Pacific. They are known for their stilt-like roots and dense canopies, playing a key role in coastal protection and supporting marine biodiversity.

Ceriops trees thrive in intertidal zones with brackish or saline waters. They are often planted along shorelines to stabilize sediments, prevent erosion, and provide habitats for fish, crustaceans, and birds.

Characteristics of Ceriops Trees

  • Medium-sized mangrove trees, 20–50 feet tall
  • Stilt or prop roots for stability in tidal, waterlogged soils
  • Leaves are thick, glossy, and salt-tolerant
  • Flowers are small, inconspicuous; produce viviparous propagules
  • Thrives in brackish and saline intertidal zones
  • Forms dense forests that provide habitat and food for coastal wildlife

Common Uses

  • Coastal protection and erosion control
  • Habitat for fish, crustaceans, birds, and other wildlife
  • Wood used for fuel, poles, and small local construction
  • Ecological restoration and mangrove reforestation projects
  • Traditional medicine in some coastal communities
