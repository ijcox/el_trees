Sour Cherry Tree
The Sour Cherry Tree (Prunus cerasus) is a medium-sized deciduous tree native to Europe and southwestern Asia. It is cultivated for its tart cherries, commonly used in cooking, baking, and preserves.

Sour Cherry trees grow 10–15 meters tall with a rounded to spreading crown. They thrive in full sun and well-drained soils. The tree produces small, bright red, tart cherries and delicate white blossoms in spring. It is valued in orchards and for ornamental purposes in gardens.

Characteristics of Sour Cherry Trees

  • Deciduous tree with ovate green leaves turning yellow in fall
  • Produces small, tart red cherries
  • White blossoms in spring
  • Moderate growth rate and moderately long-lived
  • Native to Europe and southwestern Asia, widely cultivated worldwide

Common Uses

  • Fruit for cooking, baking, preserves, and juice
  • Ornamental planting for spring flowers
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and small mammals
  • Shade tree in small to medium gardens
  • Orchard cultivation for commercial production
