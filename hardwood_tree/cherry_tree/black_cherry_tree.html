Black Cherry Tree
The Black Cherry Tree (Prunus serotina) is a medium to large deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is valued for its dark, fine-grained wood and small, dark fruit that attracts wildlife.

Black Cherry trees grow 15–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. They thrive in full sun to partial shade and well-drained soils. The tree produces small, dark purple cherries and clusters of white blossoms in spring. The wood is prized for furniture, cabinetry, and veneers.

Characteristics of Black Cherry Trees

  • Deciduous tree with ovate, finely serrated green leaves
  • Clusters of white blossoms in spring
  • Small, dark purple cherries that attract wildlife
  • Straight trunk with rounded crown
  • Moderate growth rate and long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, cabinetry, and veneers
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Ornamental planting for spring blossoms
  • Shade tree in forests and large landscapes
  • Fruit for jams, jellies, and liqueurs (in small quantities)
