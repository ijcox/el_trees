White Birch Tree
The White Birch Tree (Betula papyrifera), also known as Paper Birch, is a deciduous tree recognized for its bright white bark and smooth texture. It is native to North America and commonly found in northern forests.

White Birch trees grow 15–20 meters tall with a narrow crown and graceful branching. They prefer cooler climates and well-drained soils, often thriving near lakes and rivers. Their striking bark peels in thin layers, making them a popular ornamental choice.

Characteristics of White Birch Trees

  • Bright white bark that peels in thin layers
  • Oval to triangular green leaves
  • Slender trunk with elegant branching
  • Moderate growth rate
  • Prefers cool climates and moist soils
  • Native to North America

Common Uses

  • Ornamental landscaping tree
  • Wood used for pulp and paper production
  • Traditional use by Indigenous peoples for canoes and crafts
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Shade tree in parks and residential areas
