Black Birch Tree
The Black Birch Tree (Betula lenta), also known as Sweet Birch, is a deciduous tree valued for its dark bark and aromatic properties. It is native to eastern North America and commonly found in rich, moist forests.

Black Birch trees typically grow 18–25 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. Unlike many birch species, its bark is dark brown to nearly black and does not peel as dramatically. Twigs and bark have a strong wintergreen scent when scratched or broken.

Characteristics of Black Birch Trees

  • Dark brown to black bark with minimal peeling
  • Aromatic twigs with wintergreen scent
  • Strong, heavy wood
  • Leaves are oval with serrated edges
  • Prefers rich, moist forest soils
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture and flooring
  • Extraction of oil of wintergreen
  • Ornamental planting in landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Traditional medicinal uses
