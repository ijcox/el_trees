Silver Birch Tree
The Silver Birch Tree (Betula pendula) is a graceful deciduous tree known for its white peeling bark and delicate, airy canopy. It is native to Europe and parts of Asia and widely planted in landscapes.

Silver Birch trees typically grow 15–25 meters tall with slender branches and a light, open crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sun, often colonizing open or disturbed land. Their iconic white bark and fine foliage make them one of the most recognizable ornamental trees.

Characteristics of Silver Birch Trees

  • Distinctive white peeling bark with dark markings
  • Light, airy canopy with small triangular leaves
  • Slender, slightly drooping branches
  • Fast-growing and relatively short-lived
  • Tolerates poor soils and cooler climates
  • Native to Europe and Asia

Common Uses

  • Ornamental landscaping tree
  • Reforestation and soil stabilization
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Wood used for plywood and light construction
  • Traditional uses in medicine and crafts
