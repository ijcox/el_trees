Hickory Tree
Hickory trees are deciduous hardwood trees of the genus Carya. Native to North America and parts of Asia, they are known for their strong, dense wood and edible nuts.

Hickories thrive in temperate climates with well-drained soils. They are valued for timber, nut production, and as ornamental or shade trees in landscapes.

Characteristics of Hickory Trees

  • Deciduous trees with pinnately compound leaves
  • Produce hard, edible nuts enclosed in thick husks
  • Strong, dense wood suitable for tools, furniture, and flooring
  • Moderate to slow growth rate
  • Adapted to temperate climates with fertile, well-drained soils
  • Provide wildlife food and habitat

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, tool handles, flooring, and smoking wood
  • Edible nuts for human consumption and wildlife food
  • Ornamental planting and shade trees
  • Windbreaks and landscape restoration
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological support
