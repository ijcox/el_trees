Cherry Tree
Cherry trees are deciduous trees of the genus Prunus, famous for their delicate flowers and edible fruit. They are native to the temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere and are cultivated for ornamental and fruit-producing purposes.

Cherry trees grow well in temperate climates with well-drained soils. They are celebrated for springtime blossoms, edible cherries, and use as ornamental or shade trees in landscapes.

Characteristics of Cherry Trees

  • Deciduous trees with simple, ovate leaves
  • Produce spring blossoms in white or pink shades
  • Bear edible fruits, often red or dark red cherries
  • Moderate growth rate, adapted to temperate climates
  • Provide food and shelter for birds and insects

Common Uses

  • Ornamental trees for gardens, parks, and streets
  • Fruit production for fresh consumption, cooking, and preserves
  • Timber for furniture, cabinets, and woodworking
  • Shade and aesthetic landscaping
  • Ecological support for pollinators and wildlife
