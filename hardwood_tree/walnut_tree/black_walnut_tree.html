Black Walnut Tree
The Black Walnut Tree (Juglans nigra) is a large deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is prized for its dark, dense wood and edible nuts.

Black Walnut trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. They thrive in full sun and well-drained fertile soils. The tree produces edible nuts enclosed in a thick green husk and has pinnately compound leaves. Black Walnut wood is highly valued for furniture, cabinetry, and veneer.

Characteristics of Black Walnut Trees

  • Deciduous tree with pinnately compound leaves
  • Produces edible nuts in thick green husks
  • Dark, fine-grained, dense wood
  • Straight trunk with rounded crown
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, cabinetry, and veneers
  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Ornamental planting in large gardens and parks
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Shade tree in forests and landscapes
