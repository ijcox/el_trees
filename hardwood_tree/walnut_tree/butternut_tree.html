Butternut Tree
The Butternut Tree (Juglans cinerea), also known as White Walnut, is a medium-sized deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is valued for its edible nuts and light-colored wood.

Butternut trees grow 15–20 meters tall with a rounded crown. They thrive in full sun to partial shade and well-drained soils. The tree produces oblong, edible nuts encased in a thick, green husk and has pinnately compound leaves. The wood is used for furniture, cabinetry, and specialty woodworking.

Characteristics of Butternut Trees

  • Deciduous tree with pinnately compound leaves
  • Produces oblong, edible nuts in thick green husks
  • Rounded crown and moderately straight trunk
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Timber for furniture, cabinetry, and specialty woodworking
  • Ornamental planting in parks and gardens
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Shade tree in landscapes and forest edges
