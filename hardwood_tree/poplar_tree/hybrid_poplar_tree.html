Hybrid Poplar Tree
The Hybrid Poplar Tree (Populus × canadensis) is a fast-growing deciduous tree created by crossing different poplar species. It is commonly used in forestry and landscaping for rapid shade and timber production.

Hybrid Poplar trees typically grow 20–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. They thrive in moist, fertile soils and full sunlight. The leaves are generally triangular or diamond-shaped, and the tree produces fluffy seeds in spring.

Characteristics of Hybrid Poplar Trees

  • Triangular or diamond-shaped leaves
  • Straight trunk with broad, rounded crown
  • Fast-growing and hardy
  • Produces fluffy seeds (samaras) in spring
  • Short to moderate lifespan
  • Hybrid of multiple Populus species

Common Uses

  • Timber for pulp, plywood, and furniture
  • Rapid shade tree for parks and streets
  • Windbreaks and erosion control
  • Ornamental planting in landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
