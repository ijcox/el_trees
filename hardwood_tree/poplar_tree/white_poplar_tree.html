White Poplar Tree
The White Poplar Tree (Populus alba) is a deciduous tree known for its striking white bark and silvery leaves. It is native to Europe, central Asia, and parts of North Africa.

White Poplar trees typically grow 15–30 meters tall with a broad, spreading crown. They prefer well-drained soils and full sunlight, often growing along rivers and in floodplains. The leaves are lobed and covered with white hairs on the underside, giving a silvery appearance.

Characteristics of White Poplar Trees

  • Lobed leaves with white, hairy undersides
  • White, often mottled bark
  • Broad, spreading crown
  • Fast-growing and hardy
  • Produces small, fluffy seeds
  • Native to Europe, central Asia, and North Africa

Common Uses

  • Ornamental tree in parks and gardens
  • Shade tree for landscapes and streets
  • Timber for pulp and light construction
  • Windbreaks and soil stabilization
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
