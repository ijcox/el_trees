Carya Cordiformis Tree
The Carya Cordiformis Tree, also known as Bitternut Hickory (Carya cordiformis), is a medium to large deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is notable for its straight trunk, bitter nuts, and pinnately compound leaves.

Carya Cordiformis trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a narrow to rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sunlight. The leaves have 7 leaflets, and the nuts are small, thin-shelled, and bitter.

Characteristics of Carya Cordiformis Trees

  • Pinnately compound leaves with 7 leaflets
  • Straight trunk with narrow to rounded crown
  • Produces small, bitter nuts
  • Hard, dense wood
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for tool handles, furniture, and flooring
  • Fuelwood and smoking wood for cooking
  • Shade and ornamental tree in forests and parks
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Reforestation in upland and bottomland forests
