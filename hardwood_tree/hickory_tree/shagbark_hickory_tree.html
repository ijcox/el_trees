Shagbark Hickory Tree
The Shagbark Hickory Tree (Carya ovata) is a large deciduous tree recognized for its shaggy bark and edible nuts. It is native to eastern North America and commonly found in forests and along streams.

Shagbark Hickory trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sunlight. The leaves are pinnately compound with 5 leaflets, and the nuts are edible and sweet.

Characteristics of Shagbark Hickory Trees

  • Pinnately compound leaves with 5 leaflets
  • Shaggy, peeling bark on mature trees
  • Straight trunk with rounded crown
  • Produces edible, sweet nuts
  • Moderate growth rate and long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, tool handles, and flooring
  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Ornamental planting in large gardens and parks
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Shade tree in forests and landscapes
