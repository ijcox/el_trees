American Beech Tree
The American Beech Tree (Fagus grandifolia) is a large deciduous tree known for its smooth gray bark and impressive longevity. It is native to eastern North America and commonly found in hardwood forests.

American Beech trees can grow 20–35 meters tall with a broad, dense canopy. They prefer rich, moist, well-drained soils and are often found in mature forests. Their bark remains smooth throughout their life, making them easily recognizable.

Characteristics of American Beech Trees

  • Smooth, light gray bark that persists with age
  • Dense, spreading canopy
  • Simple, oval leaves with toothed edges
  • Produces small triangular beechnuts
  • Very long-lived species
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Shade tree in parks and forests
  • Timber for furniture and flooring
  • Wildlife food source (beechnuts)
  • Ornamental planting
  • Firewood and charcoal
