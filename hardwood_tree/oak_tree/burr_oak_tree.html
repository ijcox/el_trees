Burr Oak Tree
The Burr Oak Tree (Quercus macrocarpa) is a large deciduous oak native to central and eastern North America. It is known for its massive trunk, broad crown, and very large acorns.

Burr Oak trees can grow 20–30 meters tall with a wide, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sun to partial shade. The large acorns are an important food source for wildlife, and the durable wood is used for furniture, flooring, and construction.

Characteristics of Burr Oak Trees

  • Deciduous tree with lobed green leaves turning yellow-brown in fall
  • Massive trunk with a broad, rounded crown
  • Produces very large acorns that feed wildlife
  • Slow-growing but long-lived, often over 200 years
  • Native to central and eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and construction
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and acorn food source
  • Shade tree in open areas
  • Forest restoration and erosion control
