Red Oak Tree
The Red Oak Tree (Quercus rubra) is a large deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is known for its fast growth, strong timber, and vibrant red fall foliage.

Red Oak trees can reach 20–30 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They prefer well-drained soils and full sun and are commonly used in forests, parks, and landscaping. Acorns serve as a food source for wildlife, and the wood is valued for furniture, flooring, and construction.

Characteristics of Red Oak Trees

  • Deciduous tree with lobed green leaves turning red in autumn
  • Broad, rounded crown with strong, durable wood
  • Produces acorns that feed wildlife
  • Fast-growing and long-lived, often 200 years
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and cabinetry
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and acorn food source
  • Shade tree in open areas
  • Forest restoration and erosion control
