Oak Tree
Oak trees are large, long-lived deciduous or evergreen trees of the genus Quercus. They are widely distributed across the Northern Hemisphere and are valued for their strong timber, acorns, and ecological importance.

Oaks thrive in temperate forests, woodlands, and sometimes savannas. They are commonly used in furniture making, flooring, cabinetry, and as ornamental or shade trees.

Characteristics of Oak Trees

  • Deciduous or evergreen trees with lobed leaves
  • Produce acorns as seeds, which are important wildlife food
  • Strong, dense wood ideal for construction and furniture
  • Adapted to temperate climates and varied soil types
  • Provide habitat for birds, insects, and mammals

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, cabinetry, and barrels
  • Shade trees and ornamental planting in parks and gardens
  • Acorns for wildlife and traditional culinary uses
  • Windbreaks and erosion control in landscapes
  • Ecological support for forest biodiversity
