Balsam Fir Tree
The Balsam Fir (Abies balsamea) is a small to medium evergreen native to northeastern North America. It is known for its fragrant foliage, symmetrical shape, and use as a popular Christmas tree.

Balsam Firs typically grow 14–20 meters tall with a narrow, conical crown. They have flat, dark green needles and upright cones that disintegrate at maturity. This species thrives in cold, moist, and well-drained soils of boreal and temperate forests.

Characteristics of Balsam Fir Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with flat, soft, dark green needles
  • Upright cones that disintegrate at maturity
  • Narrow, conical crown with symmetrical shape
  • Adapted to cold, moist forests and well-drained soils
  • Lifespan typically 80–150 years

Common Uses

  • Christmas trees and ornamental planting
  • Timber for small-scale construction and furniture
  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Windbreaks and reforestation projects
  • Habitat for birds, small mammals, and insects
