Fraser Fir Tree
The Fraser Fir (Abies fraseri) is a medium-sized evergreen native to the Appalachian Mountains of the southeastern United States. It is highly prized as a Christmas tree for its pleasant scent, strong branches, and symmetrical shape.

Fraser Firs typically grow 12–18 meters tall with a dense, conical crown. They have flat, dark green needles with silvery undersides and upright cones that disintegrate at maturity. This species thrives in cool, moist, high-elevation forests.

Characteristics of Fraser Fir Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with flat, dark green needles, silvery beneath (1.5–2.5 cm long)
  • Upright cones that disintegrate at maturity
  • Dense, conical crown with symmetrical shape
  • Adapted to cool, moist, high-elevation forests
  • Lifespan typically 80–150 years

Common Uses

  • Christmas tree cultivation and ornamental planting
  • Timber for small-scale construction and furniture
  • Windbreaks and reforestation in mountainous regions
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
  • Fragrant foliage for decorative purposes
