Elm Tree
Elm trees are deciduous or semi-deciduous trees of the genus Ulmus. They are native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere and are known for their graceful shape, strong wood, and ecological importance.

Elms grow well in temperate climates with fertile, well-drained soils. They are commonly used as shade trees, street trees, and ornamental trees, though many species are threatened by Dutch elm disease.

Characteristics of Elm Trees

  • Deciduous or semi-deciduous trees with simple, alternate leaves
  • Vase-shaped or spreading crown with strong branches
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived species
  • Adapted to temperate climates and fertile soils
  • Provide habitat and food for birds, insects, and wildlife

Common Uses

  • Shade trees and street trees in urban and suburban areas
  • Ornamental planting in parks and landscapes
  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and tool handles
  • Ecological support for wildlife and forest ecosystems
  • Erosion control and windbreaks
