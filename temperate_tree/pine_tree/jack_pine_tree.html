Jack Pine Tree
The Jack Pine (Pinus banksiana) is a hardy pine native to northern North America. It is well-adapted to cold climates and poor, sandy soils, and is an important species for wildlife and forestry.

Jack Pines typically grow 9–22 meters tall with irregular, often twisted crowns. They have short needles in bundles of two and produce small cones that often remain closed until exposed to fire. This fire-adapted species is important for reforestation and provides habitat for birds, such as the Kirtland’s warbler.

Characteristics of Jack Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with short needle-like leaves in bundles of two (2–4 cm long)
  • Bark is thin, reddish-brown, and scaly
  • Produce small cones 2–4 cm long that are often serotinous (open after fire)
  • Adapted to cold climates, sandy soils, and fire-prone environments
  • Lifespan typically 50–100 years

Common Uses

  • Reforestation in northern and sandy regions
  • Timber for pulpwood and construction
  • Wildlife habitat, especially for birds and small mammals
  • Windbreaks and soil stabilization
  • Ornamental planting in cold climates
