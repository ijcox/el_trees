River Birch Tree
The River Birch Tree (Betula nigra) is a fast-growing deciduous tree known for its peeling, multi-colored bark and adaptability to wet soils. It is native to the eastern United States and commonly found along riverbanks and floodplains.

River Birch trees typically grow 12–25 meters tall and often develop multiple trunks. They thrive in moist environments and tolerate flooding better than most birch species. Their bark ranges from cream to salmon-brown and peels in thin, papery layers, adding year-round visual interest.

Characteristics of River Birch Trees

  • Peeling bark with cream, pink, and brown tones
  • Often multi-trunked growth habit
  • Leaves are diamond-shaped with serrated edges
  • Highly tolerant of wet and flooded soils
  • Fast-growing and hardy
  • Native to the eastern United States

Common Uses

  • Ornamental landscaping tree, especially near water
  • Erosion control along riverbanks
  • Shade tree in parks and large yards
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Reforestation of wetland areas
