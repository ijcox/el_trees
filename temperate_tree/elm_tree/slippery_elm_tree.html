Slippery Elm Tree
The Slippery Elm Tree (Ulmus rubra) is a deciduous tree native to North America. It is known for its inner bark, which becomes slippery when wet and has traditional medicinal uses.

Slippery Elm trees grow 12–20 meters tall with a broad, spreading crown. They prefer moist, well-drained soils and partial to full sunlight. The leaves are large, rough, and serrated, and the tree produces winged seeds (samaras).

Characteristics of Slippery Elm Trees

  • Large, rough, serrated leaves
  • Broad, spreading crown
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Inner bark becomes slippery when wet
  • Moderate growth rate and long lifespan
  • Native to North America

Common Uses

  • Traditional medicinal uses from inner bark
  • Timber for furniture and small wood products
  • Ornamental planting in landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Soil stabilization and erosion control
