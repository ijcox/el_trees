English Elm Tree
The English Elm Tree (Ulmus procera) is a tall deciduous tree native to Europe. It is known for its upright growth and was historically planted in avenues and landscapes.

English Elm trees grow 20–35 meters tall with a tall, narrow to spreading crown. They prefer fertile, well-drained soils and full sunlight. The leaves are oval with serrated edges, and the tree produces winged seeds known as samaras.

Characteristics of English Elm Trees

  • Oval, serrated leaves
  • Tall trunk with narrow to spreading crown
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Highly susceptible to Dutch elm disease
  • Native to Europe

Common Uses

  • Ornamental tree for avenues and parks
  • Shade tree in large landscapes
  • Timber for furniture and construction
  • Windbreaks in rural areas
  • Wildlife habitat
