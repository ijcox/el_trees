Pignut Hickory Tree
The Pignut Hickory Tree (Carya glabra) is a medium to large deciduous tree valued for its hard wood and bitter nuts. It is native to eastern North America and often found in upland forests.

Pignut Hickory trees grow 15–25 meters tall with a straight trunk and narrow, rounded crown. They prefer well-drained soils and full sunlight. The leaves are pinnately compound with 5–7 leaflets, and the nuts are small and bitter.

Characteristics of Pignut Hickory Trees

  • Pinnately compound leaves with 5–7 leaflets
  • Straight trunk with narrow, rounded crown
  • Produces small, bitter nuts
  • Hard, dense wood
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for tool handles, furniture, and flooring
  • Fuelwood and smoking wood for cooking
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for mammals and birds
  • Ornamental planting in forests and large landscapes
  • Shade tree in natural forests
