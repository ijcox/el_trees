Beech Tree
Beech trees are large deciduous trees of the genus Fagus. They are native to temperate regions of Europe, Asia, and North America, and are known for their smooth gray bark and dense wood.

Beech trees thrive in well-drained, fertile soils in temperate forests. They are valued for timber, ornamental planting, and their ecological role in providing wildlife habitat.

Characteristics of Beech Trees

  • Deciduous trees with simple, alternate leaves
  • Smooth gray bark and spreading canopy
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived trees
  • Adapted to temperate climates and fertile soils
  • Provide nuts (beechnuts) and habitat for wildlife

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and cabinetry
  • Ornamental planting in parks and landscapes
  • Shade trees and windbreaks
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological restoration
  • Beechnuts as wildlife food or in traditional culinary uses
