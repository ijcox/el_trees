Birch Tree
Birch trees are slender deciduous trees belonging to the genus Betula. They are native to temperate and boreal regions of the Northern Hemisphere, recognized for their striking bark and rapid growth.

Birches thrive in cool climates and well-drained soils. They are commonly used for timber, ornamental planting, and ecological restoration projects due to their adaptability and beauty.

Characteristics of Birch Trees

  • Deciduous trees with simple, alternate leaves
  • Distinctive bark: white, silver, or yellow depending on species
  • Moderate to fast growth rate
  • Adapted to temperate and boreal climates
  • Provide habitat and food for birds, insects, and wildlife

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, plywood, and cabinetry
  • Ornamental planting and landscaping
  • Shade trees for parks and gardens
  • Ecological restoration and erosion control
  • Traditional uses: sap for beverages, bark for crafts
