Blue Ash Tree
The Blue Ash Tree (Fraxinus quadrangulata) is a medium-sized deciduous tree known for its distinctive bluish-green buds and four-angled twigs. It is native to the Midwestern United States and often found in upland soils.

Blue Ash trees typically grow 15–25 meters tall with a rounded crown and sturdy trunk. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sunlight. The wood is hard and dense, and the tree produces winged seeds called samaras.

Characteristics of Blue Ash Trees

  • Compound leaves with 7–11 leaflets
  • Distinctive bluish-green buds and four-angled twigs
  • Rounded crown with a sturdy trunk
  • Produces winged seeds (samaras)
  • Moderate growth rate
  • Native to the Midwestern United States

Common Uses

  • Timber for tool handles and furniture
  • Ornamental planting in parks and yards
  • Shade tree in urban and rural areas
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Firewood and charcoal
