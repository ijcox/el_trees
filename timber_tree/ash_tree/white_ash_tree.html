White Ash Tree
The White Ash Tree (Fraxinus americana) is a large deciduous tree known for its strong, durable wood and compound leaves. It is native to eastern North America and commonly found in forests and urban landscapes.

White Ash trees typically grow 20–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sunlight. Their wood is highly valued for furniture, flooring, and baseball bats due to its toughness and elasticity.

Characteristics of White Ash Trees

  • Compound leaves with 7–9 leaflets
  • Straight trunk with rounded crown
  • Produces winged seeds called samaras
  • Strong, durable, light-colored wood
  • Moderate to fast growth rate
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and sports equipment
  • Ornamental planting in parks and streets
  • Shade tree in urban and rural areas
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Traditional medicinal uses of bark and leaves
