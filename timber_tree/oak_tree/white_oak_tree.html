White Oak Tree
The White Oak Tree (Quercus alba) is a large deciduous tree native to eastern and central North America. It is prized for its strong wood, longevity, and iconic lobed leaves.

White Oak trees typically grow 20–30 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils, full sun, and are common in forests, parks, and large landscapes. Acorns provide food for wildlife, while the durable wood is used for construction, furniture, and barrels.

Characteristics of White Oak Trees

  • Deciduous tree with lobed, green leaves that turn red or brown in fall
  • Broad, rounded crown with strong, durable wood
  • Produces acorns that feed birds and mammals
  • Long-lived, often 200–300 years
  • Native to eastern and central North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and barrels
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Erosion control and forest restoration
  • Shade tree in open areas
