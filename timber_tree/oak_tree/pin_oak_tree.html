Pin Oak Tree
The Pin Oak Tree (Quercus palustris) is a medium-sized deciduous oak native to eastern and central North America. It is known for its pyramidal shape, attractive fall color, and tolerance of wet soils.

Pin Oak trees typically grow 18–22 meters tall with a narrow, pyramidal crown. They thrive in full sun and can tolerate moist or poorly drained soils, making them ideal for urban landscapes and parks. Acorns feed wildlife, while the wood is used for furniture and general construction.

Characteristics of Pin Oak Trees

  • Deciduous tree with glossy, lobed green leaves turning red in fall
  • Narrow, pyramidal crown with strong central leader
  • Produces acorns that feed birds and mammals
  • Medium growth rate and long-lived
  • Native to eastern and central North America

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in parks, streets, and landscapes
  • Timber for furniture and construction
  • Wildlife habitat and acorn food source
  • Shade tree in urban and suburban areas
  • Erosion control in wet or poorly drained soils
