Maple Tree
Maple trees are deciduous trees of the genus Acer, known for their distinctive lobed leaves and brilliant autumn colors. They are native to the Northern Hemisphere and are highly valued for timber, syrup production, and ornamental purposes.

Maples thrive in temperate forests, urban landscapes, and well-drained soils. They are widely cultivated for their wood, maple syrup, shade, and aesthetic appeal.

Characteristics of Maple Trees

  • Deciduous trees with lobed leaves and distinctive winged seeds (samaras)
  • Vibrant fall foliage in shades of red, yellow, and orange
  • Moderate to fast growth depending on species
  • Adapted to temperate climates with well-drained soils
  • Provide habitat and food for wildlife

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and cabinetry
  • Maple syrup production from sap of Sugar Maples
  • Shade trees and ornamental planting in gardens and parks
  • Urban landscaping for streets and avenues
  • Ecological support for wildlife and forest ecosystems
