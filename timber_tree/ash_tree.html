Ash Tree
Ash trees are deciduous or semi-evergreen trees of the genus Fraxinus. They are native to temperate regions of Europe, Asia, and North America, and are prized for their strong, flexible timber and ecological benefits.

Ash trees grow well in fertile, well-drained soils with full sun to partial shade. They are commonly used for timber, shade, ornamental planting, and ecological restoration.

Characteristics of Ash Trees

  • Deciduous trees with pinnately compound leaves
  • Moderate to fast growth rate
  • Strong, flexible wood used for sports equipment and furniture
  • Adapted to temperate climates and a variety of soils
  • Provide habitat and food for wildlife

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, tool handles, and sports equipment
  • Shade trees and ornamental planting in parks and streets
  • Windbreaks and landscape restoration
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological support
  • Traditional uses in crafting and cultural applications
