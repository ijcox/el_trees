Japanese Walnut Tree
The Japanese Walnut Tree (Juglans ailantifolia) is a medium-sized deciduous tree native to Japan. It is grown for its edible nuts and ornamental appeal in gardens and landscapes.

Japanese Walnut trees grow 12–18 meters tall with a rounded crown. They thrive in full sun and well-drained soils. The tree produces large, edible nuts enclosed in a thick husk and has pinnately compound leaves. It is commonly planted for shade, nut production, and ornamental purposes.

Characteristics of Japanese Walnut Trees

  • Deciduous tree with pinnately compound leaves
  • Produces large, edible nuts in thick husks
  • Rounded crown and moderately straight trunk
  • Moderate growth rate, moderately long-lived
  • Native to Japan, cultivated worldwide

Common Uses

  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Ornamental planting in gardens and parks
  • Shade tree in landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Wood for small-scale woodworking and crafts
