Prosopis Glandulosa
Prosopis glandulosa, commonly known as honey mesquite, is a hardy desert tree valued for its drought resistance and ecological importance.

Prosopis glandulosa is native to arid regions of North America, especially the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. It is well adapted to dry environments and plays an important role in stabilizing soils and providing habitat in desert ecosystems.

The tree produces long seed pods that are consumed by wildlife and have historically been used as a food source by humans.

Characteristics of Prosopis Glandulosa

  • Drought-resistant desert tree
  • Deep taproot system
  • Produces edible seed pods
  • Thorny branches
  • Small bipinnate leaves
  • Fixes nitrogen in soil

Common Uses

  • Firewood and charcoal production
  • Animal forage
  • Soil stabilization
  • Traditional food source
  • Ecological restoration
