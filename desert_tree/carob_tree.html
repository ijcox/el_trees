Carob Tree
The Carob Tree (Ceratonia siliqua) is an evergreen tree native to the Mediterranean region, widely cultivated for its edible pods and ornamental value. Carob pods are often used as a chocolate substitute and in traditional foods.

Carob trees thrive in sunny, well-drained soils and can tolerate drought and poor soils. They are commonly planted in orchards, gardens, and urban landscapes for shade, food, and ecological benefits.

Characteristics of Carob Trees

  • Evergreen tree, 30–50 feet tall with dense canopy
  • Leaves are pinnate, dark green, glossy
  • Flowers are small, reddish-brown, inconspicuous
  • Pods are edible, elongated, sweet, and rich in sugars
  • Drought-tolerant and slow to moderate growth
  • Long-lived and resilient to poor soil conditions

Common Uses

  • Food: carob powder, sweets, and chocolate substitute
  • Ornamental and shade tree in gardens and urban areas
  • Animal feed from pods and leaves
  • Soil stabilization and erosion control
  • Traditional medicine in Mediterranean cultures
