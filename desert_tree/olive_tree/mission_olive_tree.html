Mission Olive Tree
The Mission Olive Tree (Olea europaea 'Mission') is a cultivar developed in California by Spanish missionaries. It is used for both table olives and olive oil production and is widely grown in North America.

Mission Olive trees grow 8–10 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sunlight. Leaves are evergreen, narrow, and silvery-green, and the tree produces medium-sized, oval, purple to black olives.

Characteristics of Mission Olive Trees

  • Evergreen, narrow, silvery-green leaves
  • Broad, rounded crown
  • Produces medium-sized, purple to black olives
  • Slow to medium growth, long-lived tree
  • Thrives in well-drained soils and full sun
  • Developed by Spanish missionaries in California

Common Uses

  • Table olives, especially in Mediterranean cuisine
  • Olive oil production
  • Ornamental planting in gardens and landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and insects
  • Traditional and cultural planting in California and other regions
