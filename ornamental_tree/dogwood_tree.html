Dogwood Tree
Dogwood trees (Cornus species) are deciduous or evergreen trees native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are well-known for their showy flowers, attractive foliage, and bright red berries, making them popular ornamental trees.

Dogwood trees grow best in temperate climates with well-drained soil, partial shade, and moderate moisture. They are commonly used in gardens, parks, and landscapes for decorative purposes and wildlife support.

Characteristics of Dogwood Trees

  • Small to medium-sized trees, 15–30 feet tall
  • Produces clusters of showy flowers in spring (white, pink, or red)
  • Fruit is a small red or blue drupe attractive to birds
  • Leaves are simple, ovate, and often turn reddish in autumn
  • Slow-growing with a rounded or spreading canopy

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and streetscapes
  • Attracts birds and pollinators with flowers and fruit
  • Shade and aesthetic landscaping
  • Wood used occasionally for small tools and crafts
  • Cultural and historical plantings in traditional gardens
