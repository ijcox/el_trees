Oleander Tree
Oleander (Nerium oleander) is an evergreen shrub or small tree native to the Mediterranean region. It is widely cultivated for its attractive, fragrant flowers and hardy growth, often used as an ornamental plant in warm climates.

Oleander trees grow best in full sunlight with well-drained soil and are drought-tolerant once established. They are commonly planted in gardens, streetscapes, and parks for color and visual interest.

Characteristics of Oleander Trees

  • Evergreen shrub or small tree, typically 6–20 feet tall
  • Long, narrow, leathery leaves
  • Flowers bloom in clusters, with colors ranging from white to pink to red
  • Highly drought-tolerant and resilient in hot climates
  • All parts of the plant are toxic if ingested

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and streets
  • Hedges and privacy screens
  • Container planting and decorative landscaping
  • Attracts pollinators such as bees and butterflies
  • Low-maintenance, drought-tolerant landscaping in arid regions
