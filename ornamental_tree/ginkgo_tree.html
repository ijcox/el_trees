Ginkgo Tree
Ginkgo trees (Ginkgo biloba) are deciduous conifer-like trees native to China. They are famous for their unique fan-shaped leaves, remarkable resilience, and long lifespan, often exceeding a thousand years.

Ginkgo trees thrive in temperate climates with full sunlight and well-drained soil. They are commonly planted in urban environments for shade, aesthetic appeal, and pollution tolerance.

Characteristics of Ginkgo Trees

  • Deciduous trees with fan-shaped, bright green leaves
  • Height ranges from 50–80 feet with a pyramidal to spreading canopy
  • Leaves turn golden yellow in autumn before falling
  • Slow to moderate growth rate with long-lived branches
  • Highly resistant to disease, pests, and urban pollution

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in streets, parks, and gardens
  • Shade tree in urban and residential landscapes
  • Medicinal uses: ginkgo leaf extract for cognitive and circulatory health
  • Specimen tree for aesthetic and historical appeal
  • Survivor tree in challenging urban environments due to resilience
