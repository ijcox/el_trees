Crabapple Tree
Crabapple trees (Malus species) are small to medium-sized deciduous trees native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are known for their attractive spring blossoms, colorful fruits, and use as ornamental or fruit-bearing trees.

Crabapple trees thrive in temperate climates with well-drained soil and full sunlight. They are commonly planted in gardens, parks, and streets for ornamental value and wildlife support.

Characteristics of Crabapple Trees

  • Deciduous trees with rounded or spreading crowns
  • Height typically 15–25 feet, depending on variety
  • Produce clusters of showy blossoms in spring (white, pink, or red)
  • Fruit is small, usually less than 2 inches in diameter, attractive to birds
  • Leaves often turn yellow or red in autumn

Common Uses

  • Ornamental landscaping for gardens, parks, and streets
  • Fruit used for jellies, preserves, and cider
  • Attracts pollinators and wildlife
  • Shade tree in smaller spaces
  • Occasional use in small woodworking or decorative projects
