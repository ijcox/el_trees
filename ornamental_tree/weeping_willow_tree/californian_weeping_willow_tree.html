Californian Weeping Willow Tree
The Californian Weeping Willow Tree (Salix babylonica 'Californica') is a cultivated variety prized for its ornamental weeping branches and adaptability to the Californian climate. It is commonly planted along water bodies and in parks for visual appeal.

Californian Weeping Willow trees grow 10–25 meters tall with a broad, drooping crown. They thrive in moist soils, tolerate warm climates, and prefer full sunlight. The leaves are narrow and lance-shaped, and the long branches sweep elegantly toward the ground.

Characteristics of Californian Weeping Willow Trees

  • Narrow, lance-shaped leaves
  • Broad, drooping crown with ornamental cascading branches
  • Fast-growing and medium-lived
  • Thrives in moist soils and warm climates
  • Ornamental variety cultivated for California
  • Excellent for visual landscaping near water

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting along lakes, rivers, and gardens
  • Shade tree in parks and open landscapes
  • Soil stabilization near water bodies
  • Wildlife habitat
  • Cut branches used for decorative purposes
