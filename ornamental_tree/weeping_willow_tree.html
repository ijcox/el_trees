Weeping Willow Tree
The Weeping Willow (Salix babylonica) is a large deciduous tree native to China, widely cultivated around the world for its graceful, drooping branches and ornamental appeal.

Weeping Willows thrive in temperate climates with full sunlight and moist, well-drained soil. They are often planted near water features, lakes, and parks for their dramatic cascading branches and shade.

Characteristics of Weeping Willow Trees

  • Deciduous trees with slender, drooping branches
  • Can grow 30–50 feet tall and 40–60 feet wide
  • Leaves are narrow, lance-shaped, and light green
  • Flowers appear as catkins in early spring
  • Fast-growing and long-lived under proper conditions

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting along water features and parks
  • Shade tree for large gardens
  • Wildlife habitat and shelter for birds
  • Occasional use of branches for crafts or basket weaving
  • Soil stabilization near riverbanks and lakes
