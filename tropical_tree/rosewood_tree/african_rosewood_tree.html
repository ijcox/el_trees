African Rosewood Tree
The African Rosewood Tree (Pterocarpus erinaceus) is a valuable hardwood species native to West Africa, known for its strength, durability, and warm reddish-brown wood.

African Rosewood trees grow up to 12–20 meters tall with a spreading crown and sturdy trunk. They thrive in savanna and dry forest regions with well-drained soils. This species is widely used locally and internationally, though it faces pressure from overharvesting.

Characteristics of African Rosewood Trees

  • Reddish-brown hardwood with interlocking grain
  • Strong and highly durable wood
  • Moderate density compared to other rosewoods
  • Native to West African regions
  • Adapted to drier climates
  • Resistant to termites and decay

Common Uses

  • Furniture and cabinetry
  • Flooring and construction
  • Musical instruments and drum making
  • Carvings and traditional crafts
  • Fuelwood and charcoal in local regions
