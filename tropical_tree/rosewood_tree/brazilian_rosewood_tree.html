Brazilian Rosewood Tree
The Brazilian Rosewood Tree (Dalbergia nigra) is a rare and highly prized tropical hardwood native to Brazil, known for its rich color, dramatic grain, and strong fragrance.

Brazilian Rosewood trees grow up to 20–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and spreading canopy. They thrive in tropical forests with well-drained soils and warm climates. Due to overharvesting, this species is now protected and considered endangered.

Characteristics of Brazilian Rosewood Trees

  • Rich brown to purplish wood with dark streaks
  • Highly fragrant when cut
  • Dense and durable hardwood
  • Distinctive and decorative grain patterns
  • Native to Brazil
  • Endangered due to overharvesting

Common Uses

  • Luxury furniture and cabinetry
  • High-end musical instruments (guitars, pianos)
  • Decorative veneers and paneling
  • Fine carvings and specialty items
  • Collector-grade woodworking materials
