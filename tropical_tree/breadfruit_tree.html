Breadfruit Tree
Breadfruit trees (Artocarpus altilis) are tropical trees native to the South Pacific and Southeast Asia. They are valued for their starchy, edible fruit and are widely cultivated as a staple food crop in tropical regions.

Breadfruit trees thrive in tropical climates with well-drained soils and full sun exposure. They are fast-growing and can reach large sizes, providing shade, fruit, and timber.

Characteristics of Breadfruit Trees

  • Evergreen tropical trees with large, glossy leaves
  • Produce starchy, round or oval fruits, typically 2–5 kg in weight
  • Fast-growing, reaching 30–90 feet tall
  • Adapted to humid, tropical climates
  • Provide shade, food, and wood for construction

Common Uses

  • Fruit consumption: boiled, baked, roasted, or fried as a staple food
  • Ornamental planting and shade in tropical gardens and parks
  • Timber for light construction, furniture, and crafts
  • Ecological support for pollinators and wildlife
  • Cultural significance in Pacific and tropical communities
