American Kapok Tree
The American Kapok Tree (Ceiba pentandra var. americana) is a large tropical tree native to Central and South America, known for its towering trunk, buttress roots, and cotton-like seed fibers.

American Kapok trees can grow over 60 meters tall with massive trunks and buttress roots for stability. They thrive in tropical rainforest climates with full sunlight and well-drained soils. The tree produces large, white cotton-like fibers surrounding its seeds, which are used for stuffing mattresses and pillows.

Characteristics of American Kapok Trees

  • Extremely tall trunk with broad buttress roots
  • Thrives in tropical rainforest climates
  • Produces cotton-like seed fibers
  • Supports tropical wildlife in its canopy
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Native to Central and South America

Common Uses

  • Filling material for mattresses, pillows, and cushions
  • Ornamental planting in tropical gardens
  • Habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Traditional medicinal uses of bark and leaves
  • Timber for light construction and crafts
