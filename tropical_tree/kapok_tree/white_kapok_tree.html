White Kapok Tree
The White Kapok Tree (Ceiba pentandra var. alba) is a tropical tree distinguished by its pale bark and cotton-like seed fibers, native to various tropical regions.

White Kapok trees grow 50–60 meters tall with buttress roots and a broad canopy. They thrive in tropical climates with full sunlight and well-drained soils. The soft, white fibers surrounding the seeds are widely used for stuffing and insulation, while the tree supports wildlife in tropical forests.

Characteristics of White Kapok Trees

  • Tall trunk with expansive buttress roots
  • Thrives in tropical climates
  • Produces soft, white cotton-like seed fibers
  • Supports birds and mammals
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Native to various tropical regions

Common Uses

  • Filling material for mattresses, pillows, and cushions
  • Light timber for crafts and construction
  • Ornamental planting in tropical areas
  • Wildlife habitat support
  • Traditional medicinal uses of bark and leaves
