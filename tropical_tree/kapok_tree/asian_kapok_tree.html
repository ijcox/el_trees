Asian Kapok Tree
The Asian Kapok Tree (Ceiba pentandra var. asiatica) is a tropical tree native to Southeast Asia, recognized for its tall stature, buttress roots, and soft, cotton-like seed fibers.

Asian Kapok trees grow 50–60 meters tall with a strong trunk and expansive buttress roots. They thrive in tropical climates with ample sunlight and well-drained soils. The cotton-like fibers surrounding the seeds are used for stuffing, insulation, and other purposes, while the tree supports local biodiversity.

Characteristics of Asian Kapok Trees

  • Very tall trunk with wide buttress roots
  • Thrives in Southeast Asian tropical climates
  • Produces soft, cotton-like seed fibers
  • Provides habitat for birds and mammals
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Native to Southeast Asia

Common Uses

  • Filling material for mattresses, pillows, and cushions
  • Light timber for crafts and construction
  • Ornamental planting in tropical areas
  • Wildlife habitat
  • Traditional medicinal uses of bark and leaves
