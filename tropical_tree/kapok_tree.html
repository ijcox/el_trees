Kapok Tree
Kapok trees (Ceiba pentandra) are large tropical trees native to Central and South America, West Africa, and Southeast Asia. They are famous for their cotton-like seed fibers, enormous trunks, and towering canopy in tropical rainforests.

Kapok trees thrive in tropical climates with well-drained soils and high humidity. They are ecologically important, providing habitat and food for many rainforest species, and are also cultivated for fiber and ornamental purposes.

Characteristics of Kapok Trees

  • Large, deciduous or semi-deciduous tropical trees with massive trunks
  • Produce cotton-like fibers surrounding seeds, used in stuffing and insulation
  • Fast-growing and can reach 150–200 feet in height
  • Adapted to tropical rainforest climates
  • Provide ecological habitat and food for birds, mammals, and insects

Common Uses

  • Kapok fiber for mattresses, cushions, insulation, and upholstery
  • Ornamental planting and shade in tropical gardens and parks
  • Timber used locally for light construction and canoes
  • Ecological support for rainforest ecosystems
  • Cultural and religious significance in some tropical regions
