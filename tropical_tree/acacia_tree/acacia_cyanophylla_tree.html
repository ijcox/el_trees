Acacia Cyanophylla Tree
Acacia Cyanophylla, commonly known as the Blue-leaf Acacia or Mimosa, is a fast-growing, drought-tolerant tree native to Australia. It is widely planted for ornamental purposes, erosion control, and as a nitrogen-fixing species in arid and semi-arid regions.

Acacia Cyanophylla grows best in full sunlight and well-drained soils. It is highly adaptable to poor soils and dry climates and is often used in landscaping, reforestation, and as a windbreak.

Characteristics of Acacia Cyanophylla

  • Fast-growing evergreen tree, 20–40 feet tall
  • Blue-green, pinnate leaves; feathery appearance
  • Yellow, fragrant, spherical flower clusters in spring
  • Seed pods are long, brown, and slightly curved
  • Drought-tolerant and hardy in poor soils
  • Rapid canopy formation for shade and windbreak

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and streets
  • Erosion control and land stabilization in dry areas
  • Windbreaks and shelterbelts
  • Nitrogen-fixing to improve soil fertility
  • Traditional medicine and forage for livestock
