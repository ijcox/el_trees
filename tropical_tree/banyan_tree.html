Banyan Tree
Banyan trees (Ficus benghalensis) are large, tropical fig trees native to India and Southeast Asia. They are known for their expansive canopy and aerial prop roots that form secondary trunks, creating a characteristic multi-trunk structure.

Banyan trees thrive in tropical and subtropical climates with well-drained soils and full sun to partial shade. They are culturally significant, often considered sacred in many traditions, and are widely planted in parks, temples, and open spaces for shade and ornamental purposes.

Characteristics of Banyan Trees

  • Large, evergreen tropical trees with wide-spreading canopy
  • Aerial prop roots grow into secondary trunks, forming extensive multi-trunk structures
  • Produce small figs that are consumed by wildlife
  • Adapted to tropical and subtropical climates
  • Provide extensive shade, ecological habitat, and ornamental value

Common Uses

  • Ornamental and shade trees in parks, streets, and temples
  • Cultural and religious significance in many regions
  • Wildlife habitat for birds, bats, and insects
  • Timber and leaves used locally for traditional crafts
  • Soil stabilization and ecological support in tropical regions
