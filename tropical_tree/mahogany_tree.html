Mahogany Tree
Mahogany trees are large tropical hardwood trees, primarily of the genus Swietenia. They are native to Central and South America, as well as parts of Africa, and are highly prized for their deep-colored, durable timber.

Mahogany thrives in tropical climates with well-drained soils and ample rainfall. It is widely used for high-quality furniture, cabinetry, musical instruments, and decorative woodworking.

Characteristics of Mahogany Trees

  • Tropical hardwood with deep reddish-brown wood
  • Deciduous or semi-evergreen with pinnate leaves
  • Can reach heights of 100–150 feet or more
  • Dense, straight-grained, and durable timber
  • Provides shade, ornamental appeal, and ecological habitat

Common Uses

  • High-quality furniture, cabinetry, and veneers
  • Musical instruments such as guitars and pianos
  • Boat building and interior woodwork
  • Ornamental planting in tropical regions
  • Ecological habitat for wildlife in tropical forests
