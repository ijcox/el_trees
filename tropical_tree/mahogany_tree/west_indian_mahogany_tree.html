West Indian Mahogany Tree
The West Indian Mahogany Tree (Swietenia mahagoni) is a tropical hardwood tree native to the Caribbean. It is highly prized for its reddish-brown wood, durability, and fine texture.

West Indian Mahogany trees grow 20–35 meters tall with a straight trunk and dense, rounded crown. They thrive in tropical, well-drained soils with full sunlight. The leaves are pinnately compound, and the tree produces small winged seeds in capsules.

Characteristics of West Indian Mahogany Trees

  • Pinnately compound leaves
  • Straight trunk with dense, rounded crown
  • Produces small winged seeds in capsules
  • Reddish-brown, fine-textured, durable wood
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived
  • Native to the Caribbean

Common Uses

  • High-quality timber for furniture, cabinetry, and musical instruments
  • Boat building and flooring
  • Ornamental planting in tropical landscapes
  • Shade tree in parks and plantations
  • Wildlife habitat in tropical forests
