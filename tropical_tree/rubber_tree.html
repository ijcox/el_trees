Rubber Tree
Rubber trees (Hevea brasiliensis) are tropical trees native to the Amazon rainforest. They are cultivated worldwide in tropical regions for natural latex, which is the primary source of natural rubber.

Rubber trees thrive in well-drained soils with high rainfall and full sun. They are typically grown in plantations for latex production, but also serve ornamental and ecological purposes.

Characteristics of Rubber Trees

  • Evergreen tropical trees with smooth, grayish-brown bark
  • Produce latex sap from bark for natural rubber production
  • Moderate growth, reaching 50–80 feet tall in plantations
  • Adapted to humid, tropical climates with well-drained soils
  • Provide shade, latex, and ecological support

Common Uses

  • Latex extraction for natural rubber production (tires, gloves, industrial products)
  • Ornamental planting in tropical gardens and parks
  • Timber used for furniture, flooring, and plywood
  • Ecological role in tropical ecosystems
  • Research into alternative rubber sources and sustainable latex harvesting
