South American Balsa Tree
The South American Balsa Tree (Ochroma pyramidale var. americana) is a tropical tree native to South America, widely cultivated for its extremely lightweight and buoyant wood.

South American Balsa trees grow 25–30 meters tall with straight trunks and large leaves. They thrive in humid tropical climates with well-drained soils. The wood is highly prized for model making, lightweight structures, and flotation devices due to its low density and strength.

Characteristics of South American Balsa Trees

  • Fast-growing tree reaching 25–30 meters tall
  • Thrives in South American tropical climates
  • Extremely lightweight, buoyant wood
  • Straight trunk with large leaves
  • Supports tropical forest biodiversity
  • Short-lived but rapidly renewable

Common Uses

  • Lightweight wood for models, crafts, and construction
  • Floats, rafts, and boats
  • Ornamental planting in tropical areas
  • Experimental timber for engineering and design
  • Habitat support for forest wildlife
