Himalayan Banyan Tree
The Himalayan Banyan Tree (Ficus benghalensis var. himalayana) is a fig tree native to the Himalayan region, known for its extensive aerial roots and ability to thrive in subtropical mountainous climates.

Himalayan Banyan trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a broad, spreading canopy supported by numerous aerial prop roots. They thrive in subtropical climates with well-drained soils and moderate sunlight. This variety is valued for shade, cultural significance, and supporting local biodiversity.

Characteristics of Himalayan Banyan Trees

  • Medium to large tree with extensive canopy
  • Numerous aerial prop roots forming secondary trunks
  • Thrives in subtropical mountainous climates
  • Provides dense shade
  • Supports local wildlife
  • Native to the Himalayan region

Common Uses

  • Shade and ornamental planting
  • Cultural and religious significance
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
  • Soil stabilization on slopes and terraces
  • Urban landscaping and parks
