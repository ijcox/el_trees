Mangrove Tree
Mangrove trees are specialized coastal trees and shrubs that grow in tropical and subtropical tidal regions. They are adapted to saltwater environments and play a critical role in protecting shorelines and supporting unique ecosystems.

Mangroves thrive in intertidal zones where other trees cannot survive. They have specialized root systems, such as prop roots and pneumatophores, that allow them to anchor in unstable soils and absorb oxygen in waterlogged conditions.

Characteristics of Mangrove Trees

  • Adapted to coastal and tidal saltwater environments
  • Specialized root systems (prop roots, pneumatophores) for stability and oxygen intake
  • Resistant to saline and waterlogged soils
  • Provide critical habitat for fish, birds, and other wildlife
  • Help prevent coastal erosion and buffer storm surges

Common Uses

  • Shoreline stabilization and erosion control
  • Wildlife habitat and breeding grounds for fish and birds
  • Source of timber, firewood, and traditional medicines in some regions
  • Protection against storm surges and coastal flooding
  • Ecotourism and education on coastal ecosystems
