Lebanon Cedar Tree
The Lebanon Cedar (Cedrus libani) is a majestic evergreen native to the mountains of Lebanon, Syria, and Turkey. It is famous for its historical significance, aromatic wood, and broad, spreading crown.

Lebanon Cedars grow 30–40 meters tall with a wide, spreading crown. They have needle-like leaves arranged in clusters and produce barrel-shaped cones. This species thrives in mountainous regions with well-drained soils and a temperate climate.

Characteristics of Lebanon Cedar Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in clusters
  • Produce barrel-shaped cones
  • Broad, spreading crown with a majestic appearance
  • Adapted to mountainous regions with well-drained soils
  • Long-lived, often hundreds of years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, furniture, and ships
  • Ornamental planting in parks and historical sites
  • Essential oils and resin for perfumes and incense
  • Historical and cultural significance in Lebanon and the Mediterranean
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological restoration
