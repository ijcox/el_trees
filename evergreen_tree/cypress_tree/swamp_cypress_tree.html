Swamp Cypress Tree
The Swamp Cypress Tree (Taxodium distichum) is a deciduous conifer native to the southeastern United States. It is specially adapted to grow in wetlands, swamps, and along riverbanks, often forming iconic “knees” above the water.

Swamp Cypress trees can grow 30–40 meters tall and develop wide, fluted trunks. They thrive in waterlogged soils, and their root “knees” help stabilize the tree and may aid in oxygen exchange. The foliage is soft and feathery, turning a rich orange-brown before falling in autumn.

Characteristics of Swamp Cypress Trees

  • Deciduous conifer that sheds needles in autumn
  • Soft, feathery foliage turning orange-brown in fall
  • Develops distinctive root “knees” in wet conditions
  • Highly tolerant of flooding and swamp environments
  • Native to the southeastern United States

Common Uses

  • Wetland and floodplain restoration
  • Ornamental planting in parks and gardens
  • Timber for outdoor construction
  • Wildlife habitat in swamp ecosystems
  • Shade tree in large landscapes and waterways
