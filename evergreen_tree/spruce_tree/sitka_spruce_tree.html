Sitka Spruce Tree
The Sitka Spruce (Picea sitchensis) is a tall, fast-growing evergreen native to the west coast of North America. It is highly valued for timber, pulpwood, and as an ornamental tree in large landscapes.

Sitka Spruces can grow 50–70 meters tall, making them among the tallest spruce species. They have needle-like, dark green leaves and produce slender, cylindrical cones. This species thrives in coastal climates and is widely used in construction, especially for aircraft and musical instruments due to its strong, straight-grained wood.

Characteristics of Sitka Spruce Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like, four-sided leaves (1.5–3 cm long)
  • Bark is thin and scaly, gray-brown in color
  • Produce cylindrical cones 5–10 cm long
  • Adapted to coastal climates, moist soils, and cool temperatures
  • Lifespan typically 200–500 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, aircraft, and furniture
  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Windbreaks and reforestation projects
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
