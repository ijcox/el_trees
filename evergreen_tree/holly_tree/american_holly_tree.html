American Holly Tree
The American Holly Tree (Ilex opaca) is an evergreen tree native to eastern and southeastern United States, known for its spiny leaves and bright red berries.

American Holly trees grow 15–30 meters tall with a dense, pyramidal crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full to partial sunlight. Leaves are glossy, dark green, and spiny, and the tree produces small white flowers followed by bright red berries that attract birds.

Characteristics of American Holly Trees

  • Evergreen, glossy, dark green, spiny leaves
  • Dense, pyramidal crown
  • Produces small white flowers and bright red berries
  • Slow-growing, long-lived, and shade-tolerant
  • Thrives in well-drained soils and full to partial sun
  • Native to eastern and southeastern United States

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and streets
  • Hedges and privacy screens
  • Decorative holiday arrangements using berries and foliage
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and insects
  • Shade and understory tree in forests
