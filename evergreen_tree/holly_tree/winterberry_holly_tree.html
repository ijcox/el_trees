Winterberry Holly Tree
The Winterberry Holly Tree (Ilex verticillata) is a deciduous holly native to eastern North America, noted for its bright red berries that persist through winter after the leaves fall.

Winterberry Holly trees grow 2–5 meters tall with an upright, rounded crown. They thrive in moist to wet soils and full to partial sunlight. Leaves are deciduous, turning yellow in autumn, and the tree produces clusters of small white flowers followed by bright red berries that persist through winter.

Characteristics of Winterberry Holly Trees

  • Deciduous leaves that turn yellow in autumn
  • Upright, rounded crown
  • Produces small white flowers and bright red berries
  • Medium-sized, long-lived shrub or small tree
  • Thrives in moist to wet soils and full to partial sun
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and wetlands
  • Hedges and screens in wetland or rain gardens
  • Decorative winter arrangements using berries
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and insects
  • Shade and understory planting in riparian areas
