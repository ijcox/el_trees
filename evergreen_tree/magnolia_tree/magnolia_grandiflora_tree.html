Magnolia Grandiflora Tree
The Magnolia Grandiflora Tree (Magnolia grandiflora) is a large evergreen tree native to the southeastern United States, famous for its enormous, fragrant white flowers and glossy leaves.

Magnolia Grandiflora trees grow 15–30 meters tall with a pyramidal to rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained, acidic soils and full sunlight. Leaves are broad, glossy, and evergreen, and flowers are large, showy, and fragrant, typically appearing in late spring to summer.

Characteristics of Magnolia Grandiflora Trees

  • Evergreen, broad, glossy leaves
  • Pyramidal to rounded crown
  • Large, fragrant white flowers in late spring to summer
  • Slow to medium growth rate, long-lived
  • Thrives in well-drained, acidic soils and full sun
  • Native to southeastern United States

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in large gardens, parks, and streets
  • Fragrant flowers for landscaping and floral arrangements
  • Timber for furniture and decorative woodwork
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and pollinators
  • Shade and screening in landscapes
