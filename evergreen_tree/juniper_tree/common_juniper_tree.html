Common Juniper Tree
The Common Juniper Tree (Juniperus communis) is a widely distributed evergreen conifer native to the Northern Hemisphere. It is recognized for its needle-like foliage and bluish berry-like cones.

Common Juniper trees typically grow 2–10 meters tall, but some forms can reach 15 meters. They are hardy, drought-tolerant, and often found on rocky slopes, hills, and open forests. The small blue cones are used in culinary applications, including flavoring gin.

Characteristics of Common Juniper Trees

  • Evergreen conifer with needle-like foliage
  • Small, berry-like cones that are blue when ripe
  • Variable growth habit: shrubs to small trees
  • Hardy and drought-tolerant
  • Native to the Northern Hemisphere

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting and hedges
  • Flavoring for gin and culinary uses
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
  • Soil stabilization on rocky slopes
  • Medicinal and traditional uses in some cultures
