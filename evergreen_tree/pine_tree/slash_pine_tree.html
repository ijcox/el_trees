Slash Pine Tree
The Slash Pine (Pinus elliottii) is a fast-growing pine native to the southeastern United States. It is valued for timber, resin production, and its ability to thrive in wet and sandy soils.

Slash Pines typically grow 20–30 meters tall and have long needles in bundles of two or three. They produce medium to large cones and are adapted to fire-prone environments. This species is widely used in forestry plantations and provides important habitat for wildlife.

Characteristics of Slash Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in bundles of two or three (20–30 cm long)
  • Bark is thick, reddish-brown, and deeply furrowed
  • Produce cones 7–15 cm long with winged seeds
  • Fast-growing and tolerant of wet, sandy, or clay soils
  • Lifespan typically 100–200 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, flooring, and furniture
  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Resin and turpentine production
  • Reforestation and erosion control
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
