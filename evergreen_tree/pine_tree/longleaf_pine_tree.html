Longleaf Pine Tree
The Longleaf Pine (Pinus palustris) is a majestic evergreen native to the southeastern United States. Known for its extremely long needles and large cones, it plays a critical role in the longleaf pine ecosystem.

Longleaf Pines can grow 30–40 meters tall and live for 200–400 years. They have needles that range from 20–45 cm in bundles of three and produce large, heavy cones. These trees are well-adapted to fire-prone habitats and are an important species for wildlife, timber, and reforestation.

Characteristics of Longleaf Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with very long needles in bundles of three (20–45 cm)
  • Bark is thick, reddish-brown, and fire-resistant
  • Produce large cones 15–25 cm long containing winged seeds
  • Adapted to fire and well-drained sandy soils
  • Lifespan typically 200–400 years

Common Uses

  • High-quality timber for construction and flooring
  • Resin and turpentine production
  • Reforestation and ecosystem restoration
  • Wildlife habitat for endangered species such as the red-cockaded woodpecker
  • Ornamental planting and landscape use in large spaces
