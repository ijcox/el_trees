Redwood Tree
Redwood trees are towering evergreen conifers belonging to the genera Sequoia and Sequoiadendron. They are renowned for being the tallest and some of the oldest living trees on Earth, primarily found in California and the Pacific Northwest.

Redwoods thrive in temperate coastal forests with high moisture and mild temperatures. They have thick, reddish-brown bark, needle-like leaves, and can live for thousands of years, providing iconic forest landscapes and critical ecosystems.

Characteristics of Redwood Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like foliage
  • Can reach heights over 350 feet (Coast Redwoods)
  • Extremely long-lived, some over 2,000 years
  • Thick, fire-resistant reddish bark
  • Provide habitat for numerous wildlife species

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, construction, and decking
  • Ornamental and conservation planting
  • Tourism and natural heritage preservation
  • Ecological habitat for plants and animals
  • Carbon sequestration and climate regulation
