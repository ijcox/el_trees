Subalpine Fir Tree
The Subalpine Fir (Abies lasiocarpa) is a medium-sized evergreen native to the mountains of western North America. It is adapted to high elevations and harsh climates, making it a key species in subalpine forests.

Subalpine Firs typically grow 15–25 meters tall with a narrow, spire-like crown. They have flat, dark green needles with a silvery underside and produce upright cones that disintegrate at maturity. This species thrives in cold, snowy environments and well-drained mountainous soils.

Characteristics of Subalpine Fir Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with flat, dark green needles, silvery beneath (1.5–3 cm long)
  • Upright cones that disintegrate at maturity
  • Narrow, spire-like crown
  • Adapted to high-elevation, cold, and snowy conditions
  • Lifespan typically 250–300 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, furniture, and pulp
  • Ornamental planting in cold climates
  • Reforestation and windbreaks at high elevations
  • Habitat for birds, mammals, and other subalpine wildlife
  • Christmas tree cultivation in suitable regions
