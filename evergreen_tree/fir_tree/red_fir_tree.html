Red Fir Tree
The Red Fir (Abies magnifica) is a large evergreen native to the mountains of California and Oregon. It is prized for its tall, straight growth, symmetrical shape, and use in timber and ornamental planting.

Red Firs typically grow 40–60 meters tall with a dense, conical crown. They have flat, dark green needles with silvery undersides and produce upright cones that disintegrate at maturity. This species thrives in cool, high-elevation forests with well-drained soils.

Characteristics of Red Fir Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with flat, dark green needles, silvery beneath (2–3 cm long)
  • Upright cones that disintegrate at maturity
  • Dense, conical crown with symmetrical shape
  • Adapted to cool, high-elevation mountainous forests
  • Lifespan typically 250–300 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, furniture, and paper
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Reforestation and erosion control
  • Christmas tree cultivation in suitable regions
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and forest animals
