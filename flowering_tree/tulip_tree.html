Tulip Tree
The Tulip Tree (Liriodendron tulipifera), also known as the yellow poplar, is a large deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is renowned for its tulip-shaped flowers, tall straight trunk, and vibrant yellow autumn foliage.

Tulip Trees thrive in temperate climates with full sunlight and well-drained, fertile soil. They are commonly planted in parks, gardens, and forests for shade, ornamental value, and timber production.

Characteristics of Tulip Trees

  • Large deciduous trees, often 70–100 feet tall
  • Leaves are distinctive, four-lobed, and bright green turning yellow in autumn
  • Flowers are tulip-shaped, yellow-green with orange centers, blooming in late spring
  • Fast-growing with straight trunk suitable for timber
  • Long-lived, can reach several hundred years under ideal conditions

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in parks, gardens, and streets
  • Shade tree for large landscapes
  • Timber production for furniture, veneer, and construction
  • Wildlife habitat: attracts birds and pollinators
  • Specimen tree for aesthetic and educational purposes
