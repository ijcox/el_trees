Crape Myrtle Tree
Crape Myrtle (Lagerstroemia indica) is a deciduous ornamental tree native to Southeast Asia. It is popular for its vibrant summer blooms, attractive bark, and long flowering period in warm temperate and subtropical regions.

Crape Myrtle trees grow best in full sunlight with well-drained soil. They are widely used in landscaping, parks, and streetscapes for color, shade, and decorative appeal.

Characteristics of Crape Myrtle Trees

  • Deciduous tree with smooth, mottled bark
  • Height typically 15–25 feet depending on variety
  • Flowers bloom in clusters throughout summer (pink, red, purple, or white)
  • Leaves turn yellow, orange, or red in fall
  • Moderate growth rate with spreading or vase-shaped canopy

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and streetscapes
  • Shade and visual interest in small to medium-sized landscapes
  • Attracts pollinators such as bees and butterflies
  • Specimen tree for decorative purposes
  • Urban landscaping due to drought tolerance and disease resistance
