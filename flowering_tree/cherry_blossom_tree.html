Cherry Blossom Tree
Cherry blossom trees (Prunus serrulata and related species) are deciduous ornamental trees native to East Asia, particularly Japan, Korea, and China. They are celebrated for their spectacular springtime blooms, which range in color from white to pink, and hold significant cultural importance.

Cherry blossom trees prefer temperate climates with well-drained soil and full to partial sunlight. They are commonly planted in gardens, parks, and along streets for their stunning floral displays.

Characteristics of Cherry Blossom Trees

  • Deciduous trees with smooth bark and spreading branches
  • Height ranges from 15–25 feet depending on variety
  • Leaves emerge after flowers and often turn yellow or red in autumn
  • Flowers bloom in spring in clusters, typically lasting 1–2 weeks
  • Slow to moderate growth with a rounded or umbrella-like canopy

Common Uses

  • Ornamental landscaping in parks, gardens, and streets
  • Traditional festivals and cultural displays (e.g., Hanami in Japan)
  • Shade and visual interest in temperate gardens
  • Bonsai cultivation for decorative purposes
  • Occasional use for small woodworking from branches
