Kowhai Tree
The Kowhai Tree (Sophora species) is a group of small to medium-sized deciduous trees native to New Zealand. It is well known for its vibrant yellow, bell-shaped flowers that bloom in spring and attract native birds such as the tui.

Kowhai trees thrive in full sun and well-drained soil, and are commonly found along riverbanks, forest edges, and open landscapes. They are hardy and adaptable, making them popular ornamental trees in gardens and parks.

Characteristics of Kowhai Trees

  • Deciduous or semi-deciduous trees with small, pinnate leaves
  • Height typically ranges from 15–40 feet depending on species
  • Bright yellow, tubular flowers that bloom in spring
  • Produces long seed pods containing hard, round seeds
  • Attracts birds such as tui, bellbirds, and other pollinators

Common Uses

  • Ornamental tree valued for its striking yellow flowers
  • Attracting native wildlife and pollinators
  • Planting in gardens, parks, and restoration projects
  • Cultural significance in New Zealand as a native species
  • Occasional use of wood for small crafts and tools
