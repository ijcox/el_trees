Amelanchier Tree
Amelanchier trees (Amelanchier spp.), commonly called serviceberry or shadbush, are small deciduous trees or large shrubs native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are admired for their spring blossoms, edible berries, and vibrant fall foliage.

Amelanchier trees grow well in full sun to partial shade with well-drained soil. They are commonly planted in gardens, parks, and along streets for ornamental beauty and wildlife support.

Characteristics of Amelanchier Trees

  • Small deciduous trees or shrubs, 10–25 feet tall
  • Leaves are simple, oval to elliptical, turning orange to red in autumn
  • Flowers are small, white, appearing in spring before leaf-out
  • Fruit is small, sweet, edible berries, often dark purple or blue
  • Moderate growth rate and adaptable to various soil types

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and streetscapes
  • Wildlife-friendly landscaping: berries attract birds and mammals
  • Edible fruit for jams, pies, and fresh eating
  • Shade and aesthetic interest in small to medium-sized landscapes
  • Specimen tree or hedge planting in temperate regions
