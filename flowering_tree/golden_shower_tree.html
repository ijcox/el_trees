Golden Shower Tree
The Golden Shower Tree (Cassia fistula) is a deciduous flowering tree native to the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia. It is renowned for its cascading clusters of bright yellow flowers, which bloom profusely in late spring and early summer.

Golden Shower Trees prefer full sunlight and well-drained soil and thrive in tropical and subtropical climates. They are commonly planted as ornamental trees in gardens, parks, and along streets for their stunning floral display.

Characteristics of Golden Shower Trees

  • Deciduous tree with long, pendulous branches
  • Height typically 30–40 feet with a spreading canopy
  • Leaves are pinnate, with numerous small leaflets
  • Flowers bloom in large, cascading clusters, bright yellow in color
  • Moderate growth rate with seasonal leaf drop before flowering

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and along streets
  • Shade tree in tropical and subtropical landscapes
  • Attracts pollinators such as bees and butterflies
  • Traditional medicinal uses in some cultures (bark and pods)
  • Cultural and ceremonial significance in festivals and public spaces
