Frangipani Tree
Frangipani trees (Plumeria spp.) are small deciduous or semi-evergreen tropical trees native to Central America, the Caribbean, and South America. They are celebrated for their fragrant, colorful flowers and unique sculptural branching, commonly used in ornamental gardens and landscaping.

Frangipani trees thrive in warm, frost-free climates with full sunlight and well-drained soil. They are often grown in gardens, courtyards, and as container plants in tropical and subtropical regions.

Characteristics of Frangipani Trees

  • Small to medium-sized trees, typically 10–25 feet tall
  • Leaves are large, leathery, and clustered at branch tips
  • Flowers are fragrant, five-petaled, and range in color from white, yellow, pink, to red
  • Branches are thick, succulent, and often sculptural
  • Moderate growth rate; tolerant of drought once established

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and courtyards
  • Container planting in tropical and subtropical climates
  • Flowers used in leis, perfumes, and decorative arrangements
  • Attracts pollinators such as moths and butterflies
  • Low-maintenance tropical landscaping and specimen tree planting
