Gala Apple Tree
The Gala Apple Tree (Malus domestica 'Gala') is a beloved apple cultivar prized for its sweet, mild flavor, attractive striped coloration, and early ripening. Originating in New Zealand in the 1930s, Gala has become one of the world's most widely planted apple varieties.

Gala Apple Trees are moderately vigorous, precocious bearers that produce medium-sized fruit with a distinctive orange-red stripe over a yellow-gold background. The flesh is creamy yellow, crisp, and notably sweet with subtle vanilla-like undertones. Gala apples ripen early in the harvest season, making them one of the first fresh apples available each year. They are excellent for fresh eating and hold up well in storage for several months.

Characteristics of Gala Apple Trees

  • Medium-sized fruit with orange-red stripes over a yellow-gold background
  • Creamy yellow flesh with a sweet, mild flavor and subtle vanilla notes
  • Early season ripening (typically August–September)
  • Precocious bearer; often produces fruit within 2–3 years of planting
  • Moderate vigor with a spreading growth habit
  • Requires cross-pollination with varieties such as Fuji, Honeycrisp, or Golden Delicious

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (one of the most popular lunchbox apples)
  • Apple sauce and apple butter (naturally sweet, requires less sugar)
  • Fruit salads and snack trays
  • Juice and cider blends
  • Dried apple rings
  • Home orchards and commercial production
