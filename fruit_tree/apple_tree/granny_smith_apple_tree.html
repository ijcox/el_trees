Granny Smith Apple Tree
The Granny Smith Apple Tree (Malus domestica 'Granny Smith') is a classic apple cultivar prized for its bright green skin, tart flavor, and exceptional firmness. Discovered in Australia in 1868 by Maria Ann Smith, it has become one of the world's most recognizable and versatile apple varieties.

Granny Smith Apple Trees are vigorous, late-flowering trees that thrive in warm climates. The apples are medium to large with a distinctive bright green skin that may develop a slight pink blush in full sun. Known for their high acidity and dense flesh, Granny Smith apples hold their shape well when cooked, making them the preferred choice for pies, tarts, and baking. They also store exceptionally well, often lasting several months in proper refrigeration.

Characteristics of Granny Smith Apple Trees

  • Bright green skin that may develop a light pink blush when fully ripe
  • Firm, crisp, and dense white flesh with a sharp, tangy flavor
  • Late-season ripening (typically October–November in the Northern Hemisphere)
  • Vigorous growth habit; one of the larger apple tree varieties
  • Excellent storage life, often keeping for 4–6 months under refrigeration
  • Self-sterile; requires cross-pollination with other apple varieties such as Fuji, Gala, or Golden Delicious

Common Uses

  • Baking and pies (holds shape exceptionally well under heat)
  • Fresh eating for those who prefer tart apples
  • Hard cider production (provides acidity and structure)
  • Apple sauce and chutneys
  • Salads (resists browning longer than many other varieties)
  • Dried apple rings
