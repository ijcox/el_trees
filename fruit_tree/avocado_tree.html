Avocado Tree
Avocado trees (Persea americana) are evergreen fruit trees native to Central America and Mexico. They are widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions for their nutrient-rich fruit known as the avocado.

Avocado trees thrive in warm climates with well-drained soil and plenty of sunlight. They are commonly grown in orchards and home gardens for their fruit, which is valued for its creamy texture and healthy fats.

Characteristics of Avocado Trees

  • Evergreen trees with large glossy leaves
  • Typically grow 20–60 feet tall depending on variety and conditions
  • Produce green pear-shaped fruit with a large seed
  • Require warm temperatures and protection from frost
  • Flowers have a unique pollination cycle that alternates between male and female phases

Common Uses

  • Fresh fruit consumption
  • Guacamole and culinary dishes
  • Cooking oils extracted from avocado flesh
  • Health foods due to high healthy-fat content
  • Commercial orchard production
