Apple Tree
Apple trees (Malus domestica) are deciduous fruit trees widely cultivated across temperate regions of the world. They are one of the most important fruit crops globally and are valued for their edible fruit, ornamental blossoms, and agricultural importance.

Apple trees thrive in temperate climates with cold winters and moderate summers. They grow best in well-drained soils and full sunlight, making them common in orchards, gardens, and agricultural landscapes.

Characteristics of Apple Trees

  • Deciduous trees with broad crowns and spreading branches
  • Produce fragrant white or pink blossoms in spring
  • Fruit develops in late summer to fall depending on variety
  • Typically grow 10–40 feet tall depending on rootstock and pruning
  • Require winter chill hours for proper fruit production

Common Uses

  • Fruit consumption: fresh apples, juice, cider, and processed foods
  • Commercial orchard production and agriculture
  • Ornamental planting due to spring blossoms
  • Wildlife food source for birds and mammals
  • Wood sometimes used for smoking food and small woodworking projects
