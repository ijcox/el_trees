Japanese Plum Tree
The Japanese Plum Tree (Prunus salicina) produces large, round, juicy fruit with vibrant skin colors and a sweet-tart flavor that makes them a favorite for fresh eating and preserves. Originating in China but developed extensively in Japan, these plums are prized for their size, color, and early ripening season.

Japanese Plum Trees are vigorous, upright to spreading trees that bear large, round to heart-shaped fruit with smooth skin ranging from deep crimson and burgundy to golden-yellow and even maroon-black. The flesh is yellow to red, exceptionally juicy, and sweet with a refreshing tang that balances the sugar content. Most Japanese plums are clingstone, with flesh that adheres to the pit. Japanese plums ripen in early to mid-summer, typically 2–4 weeks before European plums. They require a pollinator partner for fruit production, as most varieties are not self-fertile. These plums thrive in warmer climates with mild winters and are prized for their ornamental spring blossoms as well as their abundant fruit.

Characteristics of Japanese Plum Trees

  • Large, round to heart-shaped fruit with vibrant red, yellow, or maroon skin
  • Juicy, sweet-tart flesh in yellow or red varieties
  • Clingstone; flesh adheres to the pit
  • Early ripening (typically June–July)
  • Vigorous growth; requires cross-pollination with another Japanese plum variety
  • Prefers warmer climates with mild winters

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (large, juicy, and flavorful)
  • Plum preserves, jams, and sauces
  • Baked desserts (tarts, crisps, and galettes)
  • Plum wine and liqueurs
  • Dried plums (though less common than European varieties)
  • Home orchards and commercial production
