European Plum Tree
The European Plum Tree (Prunus domestica) is the classic plum of European orchards, prized for its sweet, flavorful fruit, exceptional versatility, and suitability for drying into prunes. Cultivated for centuries across Europe, these plums are known for their rich flavor, firm flesh, and reliable production in temperate climates.

European Plum Trees are moderately vigorous, upright trees that produce oval to oblong fruit with skin ranging from deep purple and blue-black to golden-yellow, depending on the variety. The flesh is yellow to amber, dense, and exceptionally sweet with a rich, complex flavor that intensifies when cooked or dried. Most European plums are freestone, with pits that separate easily from the flesh. European plums ripen in mid-to-late summer and are valued for their superior drying qualities, producing the prunes commonly found in markets. They are generally self-fertile and more cold-hardy than Japanese plums, making them suitable for northern growing regions.

Characteristics of European Plum Trees

  • Oval to oblong fruit with skin in shades of purple, blue-black, or golden-yellow
  • Dense, sweet, amber-colored flesh with rich, complex flavor
  • Freestone varieties; pit separates easily from flesh
  • Mid-to-late season ripening (typically August–September)
  • Cold-hardy and reliable producer in temperate climates
  • Self-fertile; does not require a pollinator

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (sweet, rich flavor)
  • Prune production (dried plums)
  • Plum preserves, jams, and butters
  • Baking (tarts, cakes, and pastries)
  • Plum sauce and chutneys
  • Home orchards and commercial production
