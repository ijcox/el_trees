Damson Plum Tree
The Damson Plum Tree (Prunus domestica subsp. insititia) is a small, tart plum variety prized for its intense flavor, exceptional preserving qualities, and legendary use in jams, jellies, and spirits. Believed to have originated in the Near East and introduced to Europe by the Romans, Damson plums have been cultivated for centuries, particularly in England where they remain a beloved heritage fruit.

Damson Plum Trees are small, hardy, and productive, often growing as multi-stemmed shrubs or small trees. They bear small, oval fruit with deep blue-black to purple skin covered in a distinctive powdery bloom. The flesh is golden-yellow to greenish, exceptionally tart, and astringent with a rich, complex flavor that transforms beautifully when cooked. Damson plums are clingstone, with flesh that adheres tightly to the small pit. Damson plums ripen in late summer to early autumn and are rarely eaten fresh due to their astringency. Instead, they are celebrated for their high pectin content, which makes them ideal for jams, jellies, and preserves. They are also used to make damson gin, a traditional British liqueur. Damson trees are self-fertile, cold-hardy, and tolerant of poor soils.

Characteristics of Damson Plum Trees

  • Small, oval fruit with deep blue-black to purple skin and powdery bloom
  • Golden-yellow to greenish flesh that is tart, astringent, and intensely flavorful
  • Clingstone; flesh adheres tightly to the small pit
  • Late season ripening (typically August–September)
  • Compact, hardy trees; self-fertile and tolerant of poor soils
  • Exceptionally high in pectin, ideal for preserves

Common Uses

  • Damson jam, jelly, and preserves (high pectin content)
  • Damson gin (traditional British liqueur)
  • Fruit pastes and cheese
  • Chutneys and sauces
  • Baked desserts and tarts
  • Heritage orchards and home preserving
