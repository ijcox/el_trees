Fig Tree
Fig trees (Ficus carica) are deciduous fruit trees native to the Middle East and Western Asia. They are among the oldest cultivated plants in human history and are valued for their sweet, soft fruit and distinctive broad leaves.

Fig trees grow well in warm temperate and Mediterranean climates. They tolerate drought well and can thrive in poor soils, making them popular for home gardens and orchards.

Characteristics of Fig Trees

  • Deciduous trees with broad, lobed leaves
  • Usually grow 10–30 feet tall depending on pruning and variety
  • Produce soft, pear-shaped fruits with sweet flesh
  • Fruit may be green, purple, brown, or black depending on variety
  • Highly drought tolerant once established

Common Uses

  • Fresh fruit consumption
  • Dried figs used in baking and cooking
  • Production of jams, preserves, and desserts
  • Traditional medicine and nutrition
  • Ornamental planting in warm climates
