Lime Tree
Lime trees (Citrus aurantiifolia and related species) are evergreen citrus trees native to Southeast Asia. They are widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions for their small, green, acidic fruit used in cooking and beverages.

Lime trees thrive in warm climates with full sunlight and well-drained soil. They are commonly grown in orchards, gardens, and containers for both culinary and ornamental purposes.

Characteristics of Lime Trees

  • Evergreen trees with dense foliage
  • Produce small, fragrant white blossoms
  • Fruit typically green when mature, though some varieties turn yellow
  • Usually grow 10–20 feet tall depending on species
  • Often have thorny branches

Common Uses

  • Flavoring for food, beverages, and desserts
  • Production of lime juice and cocktails
  • Essential oils extracted from lime peel
  • Ornamental planting in warm climates
  • Leaves and fruit used in traditional cuisine and herbal remedies
