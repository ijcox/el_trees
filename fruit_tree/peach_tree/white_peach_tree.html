White Peach Tree
The White Peach Tree (Prunus persica var. alba) produces elegant, pale-skinned fruit with exceptionally sweet, low-acid flesh that is prized in Asian cuisines and among connoisseurs for its delicate, floral flavor. White peaches have been cultivated for centuries, particularly in China and Japan, and have gained popularity worldwide for their refined sweetness and aromatic qualities.

White Peach Trees are moderately vigorous trees that bear medium to large fruit with pale, creamy-white to blush-pink skin. The flesh is white, exceptionally sweet, and low in acid with a delicate, floral aroma and honey-like flavor that lacks the tangy bite of traditional yellow peaches. Depending on the variety, white peaches may be either freestone or clingstone. White peaches are more fragile than yellow varieties, with thinner skin and softer flesh that bruises easily. They ripen in mid-to-late summer and are best enjoyed fresh, as their delicate flavor and texture can be lost when cooked. The trees are self-fertile and produce reliably in warm climates.

Characteristics of White Peach Trees

  • Pale, creamy-white to blush-pink skin with minimal red coloring
  • White, exceptionally sweet, low-acid flesh with delicate floral, honey-like flavor
  • Softer, more fragile flesh than yellow peaches; bruises easily
  • Mid-to-late season ripening (typically July–August)
  • Moderate vigor; prefers warm climates with adequate chill hours
  • Self-fertile; does not require a pollinator

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (prized for delicate, floral sweetness)
  • Fruit platters and elegant desserts
  • Asian cuisine (desserts, teas, and fruit plates)
  • White peach sangria and cocktails
  • Artisanal preserves and sorbets
  • Home orchards and specialty markets
