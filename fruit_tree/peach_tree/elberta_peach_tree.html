Elberta Peach Tree
The Elberta Peach Tree (Prunus persica 'Elberta') is one of the most famous and widely planted peach varieties, celebrated for its large, juicy fruit, golden-yellow flesh, and exceptional canning qualities. Developed in Georgia in the 1870s by Samuel Rumph, it became the standard for commercial peach production across the United States.

Elberta Peach Trees are vigorous, productive trees that bear large, round fruit with a golden-yellow skin blushed with deep red. The flesh is yellow, exceptionally juicy, and sweet with a rich, classic peach flavor and a freestone pit that separates easily from the flesh. Elberta peaches ripen in mid-to-late summer and are prized for their versatility, performing equally well for fresh eating, canning, baking, and freezing. The trees are self-fertile and reliable producers, making them a favorite for home orchards and commercial growers alike.

Characteristics of Elberta Peach Trees

  • Large, round fruit with golden-yellow skin blushed with deep red
  • Yellow, juicy flesh with rich, sweet, classic peach flavor
  • Freestone pit separates easily from the flesh
  • Mid-to-late season ripening (typically July–August)
  • Vigorous, productive growth habit with reliable annual bearing
  • Self-fertile; does not require a pollinator

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (classic peach flavor)
  • Canning and preserving (the standard peach for home canning)
  • Peach pies, cobblers, and baked desserts
  • Freezing for year-round use
  • Peach preserves and jams
  • Home orchards and commercial production
