Blood Orange Tree
The Blood Orange Tree (Citrus sinensis 'Blood Orange') produces a striking fruit with deep crimson flesh, a unique raspberry-like flavor, and an alluring color that has made it a favorite among chefs and citrus enthusiasts. Originating in Sicily, blood oranges are prized for their anthocyanin pigments, which develop only under specific temperature conditions, giving the fruit its characteristic red blush.

Blood Orange Trees are medium-sized, evergreen trees with glossy foliage and fragrant white blossoms. They produce medium-sized, round to slightly oval fruit with skin ranging from orange to deep red-blushed, depending on the variety. The flesh is the defining feature, displaying varying degrees of red streaking to deep burgundy coloration, with a complex flavor profile that combines sweet citrus notes with distinct berry-like undertones, often compared to raspberry or strawberry. Blood oranges ripen in winter, typically from December through March, with the deepest color development occurring in regions with cool nights. Popular varieties include Moro (deepest red), Tarocco (sweetest), and Sanguinello (classic Spanish type). The trees are self-fertile and require warm days with cool nights for optimal anthocyanin development.

Characteristics of Blood Orange Trees

  • Medium-sized fruit with orange to red-blushed skin
  • Deep crimson to burgundy flesh with unique berry-like, raspberry undertones
  • Complex sweet-tart flavor profile distinct from standard oranges
  • Winter ripening (typically December–March)
  • Anthocyanin pigments develop with cool night temperatures
  • Self-fertile; thrives in Mediterranean climates

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (striking appearance and unique flavor)
  • Blood orange juice and cocktails
  • Gourmet salads and desserts
  • Sorbet, marmalade, and preserves
  • Cheese boards and culinary garnishes
  • Home orchards and specialty markets
