Navel Orange Tree
The Navel Orange Tree (Citrus sinensis 'Navel') produces the world's most popular eating orange, distinguished by its characteristic navel formation at the blossom end, seedless flesh, and exceptional sweetness. Originating from a single mutation in Brazil in the early 19th century, every Navel orange tree today is propagated from that original parent, making it one of the most successful clonal varieties in agriculture.

Navel Orange Trees are medium-sized, evergreen trees with glossy, dark green foliage and fragrant white blossoms. They produce medium to large, round fruit with bright orange, slightly pebbled skin that is easy to peel. The flesh is seedless, exceptionally sweet, and low in acid with a rich, full flavor and a distinctive navel-like secondary fruit embedded at the blossom end. Navel oranges ripen in winter, typically from November through January, and are prized as fresh eating oranges, as their flavor can become bitter when juiced due to the presence of limonin in the flesh. The trees are self-fertile, vigorous, and well-suited to Mediterranean and subtropical climates.

Characteristics of Navel Orange Trees

  • Medium to large, round fruit with bright orange, easy-peel skin
  • Seedless flesh with exceptional sweetness, low acidity, and rich flavor
  • Distinctive navel formation (secondary fruit) at the blossom end
  • Winter ripening (typically November–January)
  • Medium-sized evergreen tree with glossy foliage
  • Self-fertile; does not require cross-pollination

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (the premier dessert orange)
  • Fruit platters and snack oranges
  • Salads and fruit arrangements
  • Zest for baking and cooking
  • Home orchards and commercial fresh market production
