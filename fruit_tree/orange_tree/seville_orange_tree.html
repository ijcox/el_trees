Seville Orange Tree
The Seville Orange Tree (Citrus × aurantium), also known as sour orange or bitter orange, produces the quintessential marmalade orange, prized for its intense bitterness, high pectin content, and aromatic peel. With origins in Southeast Asia, Seville oranges were introduced to Europe by the Moors and have become legendary in British and Spanish culinary traditions.

Seville Orange Trees are medium-sized, evergreen trees with dense, dark green foliage and extremely fragrant white blossoms. They produce medium-sized, round to slightly flattened fruit with rough, thick, deep orange to reddish-orange skin that is highly aromatic. The flesh is extremely tart, bitter, and acidic with numerous seeds, making it unpalatable for fresh eating but ideal for preserves and culinary applications. Seville oranges ripen in winter, typically from December through February. They are distinguished by their exceptionally high pectin content, which allows marmalades to set without added pectin, and their intensely fragrant peel, which is prized in flavorings and liqueurs. The trees are self-fertile, cold-hardy for citrus, and often used as rootstock for other citrus varieties.

Characteristics of Seville Orange Trees

  • Medium-sized fruit with rough, thick, deep orange to reddish-orange skin
  • Extremely tart, bitter, acidic flesh with numerous seeds
  • Exceptionally high pectin content ideal for preserves
  • Highly fragrant peel with intense essential oils
  • Winter ripening (typically December–February)
  • Self-fertile; cold-hardy for citrus

Common Uses

  • Marmalade (the classic Seville orange marmalade)
  • Orange liqueurs (Grand Marnier, Cointreau, triple sec)
  • Bitter orange flavoring in sauces and marinades
  • Essential oils and perfumery
  • Traditional British and Spanish preserves
  • Rootstock for other citrus varieties
