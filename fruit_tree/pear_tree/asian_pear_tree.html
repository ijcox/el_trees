Asian Pear Tree
The Asian Pear Tree (Pyrus pyrifolia) produces a unique fruit that combines the crisp, crunchy texture of an apple with the sweet, floral flavor of a pear. Known by many names including apple pear, sand pear, and nashi, Asian pears have been cultivated in East Asia for over 3,000 years.

Asian Pear Trees are vigorous, upright growers that produce round, apple-shaped fruit with smooth, golden-yellow to bronze-russeted skin. Unlike European pears, Asian pears ripen on the tree and are eaten crisp, similar to apples, maintaining their firm texture even when fully ripe. The flesh is white, exceptionally juicy, and refreshingly sweet with a delicate floral aroma. Asian pears are prized for their long storage life, often keeping for several months under refrigeration. They are a staple fruit in many Asian cuisines and have gained popularity worldwide for their unique texture and flavor profile.

Characteristics of Asian Pear Trees

  • Round, apple-shaped fruit with smooth golden-yellow to bronze-russeted skin
  • White, crisp, juicy flesh with sweet, floral flavor and apple-like crunch
  • Ripens on the tree; harvested when ready to eat (does not soften after picking)
  • Mid-to-late season ripening (typically August–October depending on variety)
  • Vigorous growth habit; many varieties are self-fertile or partially self-fertile
  • Excellent storage life (2–4 months under refrigeration)

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (crisp texture makes it a refreshing snack)
  • Fruit salads and slaws (maintains crunch when sliced)
  • Asian cuisine (used in marinades, stir-fries, and as a palate cleanser)
  • Gift boxes and specialty markets
  • Juice and cider
  • Home orchards and commercial production
