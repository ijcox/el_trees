Bartlett Pear Tree
The Bartlett Pear Tree (Pyrus communis 'Bartlett') is the most widely grown pear variety in the world, prized for its sweet, juicy flavor, aromatic flesh, and classic bell shape. Discovered in England in the 18th century, it remains the standard against which all dessert pears are measured.

Bartlett Pear Trees are vigorous, productive trees that bear medium to large fruit with smooth, thin skin that ripens from green to golden yellow. The flesh is white, exceptionally juicy, and buttery-smooth with a distinctive sweet, aromatic flavor and subtle honey notes. Bartlett pears are unique among European pears in that they ripen on the tree, though they are typically harvested when mature but still firm and allowed to finish ripening off the tree for optimal texture and flavor. They are equally prized for fresh eating and canning.

Characteristics of Bartlett Pear Trees

  • Classic bell-shaped fruit with smooth skin that ripens from green to golden yellow
  • White, exceptionally juicy flesh with buttery texture and sweet, aromatic flavor
  • Mid-season ripening (typically August–September)
  • Vigorous, upright growth habit with heavy annual production
  • Partially self-fertile, but yields improve significantly with cross-pollination
  • Requires cross-pollination with varieties such as Anjou, Bosc, or Comice

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (classic dessert pear)
  • Canning and preserving (the standard pear for home canning)
  • Pear butter and preserves
  • Poached pears and baked desserts
  • Juice and perry (pear cider)
  • Home orchards and commercial production
