Anjou Pear Tree
The Anjou Pear Tree (Pyrus communis 'Anjou') is a classic European pear variety prized for its mild, sweet flavor, dense flesh, and exceptional storage life. Originating in France or Belgium in the 19th century, it is available in both green and red-skinned forms and remains one of the most widely grown commercial pears.

Anjou Pear Trees are vigorous, upright growers that produce medium to large fruit with a characteristic egg-shaped profile. Green Anjou pears maintain their green color even when ripe, while Red Anjou pears develop a deep burgundy blush. The flesh is dense, fine-grained, and buttery with a mild, subtly sweet flavor that lacks the intense perfume of Bartlett pears. Anjou pears are known for their remarkable storage life, often lasting 5–7 months under proper refrigeration, making them a reliable choice for extended availability.

Characteristics of Anjou Pear Trees

  • Egg-shaped fruit with smooth skin; Green Anjou remains green when ripe, Red Anjou develops deep burgundy blush
  • Dense, fine-grained flesh with mild, sweet flavor and buttery texture
  • Late-season ripening (typically September–October)
  • Vigorous, upright growth habit with consistent annual production
  • Exceptional storage life (5–7 months under refrigeration)
  • Requires cross-pollination with varieties such as Bartlett, Bosc, or Comice

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (mild, approachable flavor suitable for all palates)
  • Cheese boards and fruit platters (holds shape well when sliced)
  • Poached pears and baked desserts
  • Salads (dense flesh resists browning)
  • Long-term cold storage for winter availability
  • Home orchards and commercial production
