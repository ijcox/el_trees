Bosc Pear Tree
The Bosc Pear Tree (Pyrus communis 'Bosc') is an elegant European pear variety celebrated for its long, tapered neck, russeted brown skin, and rich, spicy-sweet flavor. Originating in Belgium or France in the early 19th century, Bosc pears are prized for holding their shape beautifully when cooked.

Bosc Pear Trees are moderately vigorous, upright growers that produce distinctive fruit with a long, graceful neck and warm cinnamon-brown skin covered in russet. The flesh is dense, crisp, and aromatic with a complex flavor profile featuring honey, spice, and subtle vanilla notes. Unlike many pear varieties, Bosc pears maintain their shape exceptionally well when exposed to heat, making them the preferred choice for poaching, baking, and other cooked applications. They also store well, developing richer flavor over several months in cold storage.

Characteristics of Bosc Pear Trees

  • Distinctive long, tapered neck with russeted cinnamon-brown skin
  • Dense, crisp, aromatic flesh with rich, spicy-sweet flavor and vanilla undertones
  • Mid-to-late season ripening (typically September–October)
  • Moderate vigor with upright, spreading growth habit
  • Exceptional heat tolerance; holds shape beautifully when cooked
  • Requires cross-pollination with varieties such as Bartlett, Anjou, or Comice

Common Uses

  • Poached pears (classic choice due to excellent shape retention)
  • Baked desserts, tarts, and galettes
  • Fresh eating (crisp texture when ripe)
  • Cheese boards (pairs exceptionally well with blue cheese and aged cheeses)
  • Roasted pear dishes
  • Home orchards and specialty markets
