Japanese Cherry Tree
The Japanese Cherry Tree (Prunus serrulata) is a small to medium deciduous tree native to Japan, Korea, and China. It is widely celebrated for its spectacular spring blossoms, often referred to as "sakura."

Japanese Cherry trees grow 6–12 meters tall with a rounded to spreading crown. They thrive in full sun and well-drained soils. The tree produces abundant pink to white blossoms in early spring, creating stunning floral displays in gardens, parks, and streetscapes.

Characteristics of Japanese Cherry Trees

  • Deciduous tree with ovate to serrated green leaves
  • Produces showy pink to white blossoms in spring
  • Small to medium-sized tree with a rounded to spreading crown
  • Moderate growth rate and moderately long-lived
  • Native to East Asia and widely cultivated worldwide for ornamental purposes

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting for spectacular spring blossoms
  • Public parks, streets, and gardens
  • Wildlife habitat and nectar for pollinators
  • Shade tree in small to medium landscapes
  • Celebratory and cultural plantings (Cherry Blossom Festivals)
