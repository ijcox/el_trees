Common Fig Tree
The Common Fig Tree (Ficus carica) is a widely cultivated deciduous fruit tree known for its sweet, pear-shaped fruits and ancient cultural significance.

Common Fig trees grow 3–10 meters tall with broad, lobed leaves and a spreading canopy. They thrive in warm, temperate to subtropical climates with well-drained soil and full sun. The fruit is soft, sweet, and consumed fresh or dried, making it one of the oldest cultivated fruit trees.

Characteristics of Common Fig Trees

  • Deciduous fruit tree with broad lobed leaves
  • Produces sweet pear-shaped figs
  • Thrives in warm temperate climates
  • Medium-sized with spreading canopy
  • Ancient cultivated species
  • Drought-tolerant once established

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating and dried fruit production
  • Baking and desserts
  • Ornamental garden planting
  • Wildlife food source
  • Traditional and historical cultivation
