Ponderosa Lemon Tree
The Ponderosa Lemon Tree (Citrus × pyriformis) produces dramatically large, bumpy-skinned fruit that can weigh up to 5 pounds, making it a novelty citrus prized for its ornamental value and thick, aromatic peel. A hybrid of lemon and citron, Ponderosa lemons originated in the late 19th century and remain popular among home gardeners for their striking appearance and cold hardiness.

Ponderosa Lemon Trees are medium-sized, evergreen trees with an open, spreading growth habit and large, glossy leaves. They produce enormous, pear-shaped to round fruit that can reach 4–5 pounds, with thick, bumpy, bright yellow skin that is heavily textured and highly aromatic. The flesh is pale yellow, moderately acidic, and contains numerous seeds, with a flavor that is more citron-like than true lemon, featuring a thick, spongy rind and relatively less juice. Ponderosa lemons are everbearing, producing fruit year-round with peak harvest in winter and spring. They are more cold-tolerant than standard lemons and are often grown for their ornamental value as much as their fruit. The trees are self-fertile and make striking specimen plants in containers or landscapes.

Characteristics of Ponderosa Lemon Trees

  • Enormous, pear-shaped to round fruit weighing up to 5 pounds
  • Thick, bumpy, bright yellow, highly aromatic skin
  • Pale yellow flesh with moderate acidity; numerous seeds
  • Thick, spongy rind with relatively less juice than standard lemons
  • Everbearing; peak harvest in winter and spring
  • Self-fertile; more cold-hardy than true lemons

Common Uses

  • Ornamental specimen tree (striking, oversized fruit)
  • Zest and candied peel (thick rind ideal for preserving)
  • Lemonade and beverages (one fruit yields substantial juice)
  • Marmalade and preserves
  • Container growing and novelty gardens
