Meyer Lemon Tree
The Meyer Lemon Tree (Citrus × meyeri) is a beloved hybrid lemon prized for its sweet, less acidic flavor, thin skin, and exceptional cold hardiness. Discovered in China by Frank Meyer in the early 20th century, Meyer lemons have become a favorite among home gardeners and chefs for their unique, aromatic flavor and compact growth habit.

Meyer Lemon Trees are small to medium, compact evergreen trees with dense, dark green foliage and a naturally bushy growth habit, making them ideal for container growing and home orchards. They produce small to medium, round to slightly oval fruit with thin, smooth, deep yellow to orange-yellow skin that is highly aromatic. The flesh is pale orange-yellow, less acidic than true lemons, with a sweet, floral, mildly tangy flavor that combines notes of lemon and mandarin orange. Meyer lemons are everbearing, producing fruit primarily in winter and spring, with some varieties flowering year-round. They are significantly more cold-tolerant than true lemons, surviving light frosts, and are self-fertile. The fruit is seedless or contains few seeds, and the thin skin makes it excellent for whole-fruit uses.

Characteristics of Meyer Lemon Trees

  • Small to medium, round to oval fruit with thin, smooth, deep yellow to orange-yellow skin
  • Pale orange-yellow flesh with sweet, floral, mildly tangy flavor
  • Less acidic than true lemons; lemon-mandarin hybrid flavor
  • Everbearing; peak harvest in winter and spring
  • Compact, bushy growth; ideal for containers
  • Self-fertile; more cold-hardy than other lemons

Common Uses

  • Fresh eating (sweet enough to eat raw)
  • Lemonade and cocktails (less tart, more complex)
  • Baking and desserts (subtle, floral flavor)
  • Preserved lemons (thin skin ideal for Moroccan cuisine)
  • Container gardening and home orchards
