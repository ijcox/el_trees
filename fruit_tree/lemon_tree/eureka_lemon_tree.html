Eureka Lemon Tree
The Eureka Lemon Tree (Citrus × limon 'Eureka') is the classic supermarket lemon, prized for its tart, acidic juice, prolific bearing, and nearly year-round harvest. Originating in California in the mid-19th century, Eureka lemons became one of the world's most commercially important lemon varieties alongside Lisbon.

Eureka Lemon Trees are medium-sized, evergreen trees with an open, spreading growth habit and sparse foliage compared to other citrus. They produce medium to large, oval fruit with moderately thick, bright yellow skin that is slightly textured. The flesh is pale yellow, highly acidic, and exceptionally juicy with a classic tart lemon flavor and contains few to no seeds. Eureka lemons are true everbearing trees, producing fruit continuously throughout the year, with peak harvest in spring and summer. They are sensitive to cold and thrive in warm, frost-free climates. The trees are self-fertile and are among the most productive lemon varieties, though they can be more susceptible to pests and diseases than some other citrus.

Characteristics of Eureka Lemon Trees

  • Medium to large, oval fruit with bright yellow, moderately thick skin
  • Pale yellow, highly acidic, juicy flesh with classic tart lemon flavor
  • Few to no seeds
  • Everbearing; produces fruit nearly year-round
  • Open, spreading growth habit with sparse foliage
  • Self-fertile; requires warm, frost-free climates

Common Uses

  • Fresh lemon juice (the standard for culinary use)
  • Lemonade and beverages
  • Cooking and baking (acidic flavor holds up to heat)
  • Zest for flavoring
  • Commercial production and home orchards
