Coast Redwood Tree
The Coast Redwood (Sequoia sempervirens) is the tallest tree species on Earth. It is native to the coastal forests of northern California and southern Oregon.

Coast Redwoods can grow over 100 meters tall and live for more than 2,000 years. They thrive in cool, moist coastal climates where ocean fog provides essential moisture. These trees form dense forests that are among the most iconic ecosystems in North America.

Characteristics of Coast Redwood Trees

  • Tallest tree species in the world
  • Evergreen conifer with soft flat needles
  • Thick reddish bark resistant to fire and insects
  • Can live over 2,000 years
  • Native to coastal California and southern Oregon

Common Uses

  • Durable lumber for construction
  • Outdoor decking and fencing
  • Ornamental planting in parks and landscapes
  • Conservation forests and national parks
  • Wildlife habitat in coastal ecosystems
