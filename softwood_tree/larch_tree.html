Larch Tree
Larch trees are deciduous coniferous trees belonging to the genus Larix. Unlike most conifers, larches lose their needles in the fall and are valued for their durable timber and ornamental qualities.

Larches grow in temperate and boreal regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They often have straight trunks, conical shapes, and vibrant yellow foliage in autumn, making them visually striking and ecologically significant.

Characteristics of Larch Trees

  • Deciduous conifers that lose needles in autumn
  • Vibrant yellow foliage during fall season
  • Conical or pyramidal shape with straight trunks
  • Adapted to temperate and boreal climates
  • Provide habitat for wildlife and support forest biodiversity

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, flooring, and furniture
  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Ornamental planting due to autumn foliage color
  • Windbreaks and reforestation projects
  • Soil stabilization and habitat support in forested areas
