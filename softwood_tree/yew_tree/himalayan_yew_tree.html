Himalayan Yew Tree
The Himalayan Yew Tree (Taxus wallichiana) is an evergreen conifer native to the Himalayan region of Nepal, India, and Bhutan. It is known for its dark green foliage, slow growth, and use in traditional medicine.

Himalayan Yew trees typically grow 10–20 meters tall with a narrow, conical crown. They thrive in shaded forests and well-drained soils at high altitudes. The red arils are eaten by birds, while the leaves and bark contain toxic compounds and medicinally important substances like paclitaxel.

Characteristics of Himalayan Yew Trees

  • Evergreen conifer with dark green, needle-like foliage
  • Narrow, conical crown, slow-growing
  • Produces red, berry-like arils surrounding seeds
  • Shade- and high-altitude tolerant
  • Native to the Himalayan region

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in Himalayan gardens
  • Wildlife habitat for birds
  • Source of paclitaxel for medicinal use
  • Conservation of native forest understory
  • Small-scale timber and specialty woodworking
