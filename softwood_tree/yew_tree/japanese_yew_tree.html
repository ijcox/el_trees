Japanese Yew Tree
The Japanese Yew Tree (Taxus cuspidata) is an evergreen conifer native to Japan, Korea, and parts of eastern Asia. It is prized for its dense, dark green foliage and adaptability in ornamental and hedge plantings.

Japanese Yew trees grow 10–18 meters tall, often with a broad, pyramidal crown. They are tolerant of shade, urban conditions, and a variety of soil types. The red arils provide food for birds, while all other parts of the plant are toxic if ingested by humans.

Characteristics of Japanese Yew Trees

  • Evergreen conifer with dark green, needle-like foliage
  • Broad, pyramidal crown with dense growth
  • Produces red, berry-like arils surrounding seeds
  • Shade- and urban-tolerant
  • Native to Japan and eastern Asia

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting, hedges, and privacy screens
  • Topiary and formal gardens
  • Wildlife habitat for birds
  • Urban landscaping and parks
  • Container planting and bonsai cultivation
