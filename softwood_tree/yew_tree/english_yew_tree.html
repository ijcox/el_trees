English Yew Tree
The English Yew Tree (Taxus baccata) is an evergreen conifer native to Europe. It is known for its dark green foliage, long lifespan, and historical use in hedges, topiary, and timber.

English Yew trees can grow 10–20 meters tall, sometimes reaching 25 meters, with a broad, spreading crown. They thrive in well-drained soils, tolerate shade, and are often found in churchyards, gardens, and forests. The bright red arils provide food for birds, while all other parts of the plant are toxic if ingested by humans.

Characteristics of English Yew Trees

  • Evergreen conifer with dark green, needle-like foliage
  • Broad, spreading crown with slow to moderate growth
  • Produces red, berry-like arils surrounding seeds
  • Shade-tolerant and long-lived, some over 1,000 years old
  • Native to Europe

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting and hedges
  • Topiary and formal gardens
  • Timber for bows and furniture
  • Wildlife habitat for birds
  • Historical plantings in churchyards and cemeteries
