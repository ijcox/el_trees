Irish Yew Tree
The Irish Yew Tree (Taxus baccata 'Fastigiata') is a distinctive columnar form of the English Yew. It is native to Ireland and widely cultivated for its elegant upright shape and dense, dark green foliage.

Irish Yew trees grow 10–18 meters tall with a narrow, columnar crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sun to partial shade, making them popular in formal gardens, cemeteries, and hedges. The red arils feed birds, while other parts of the tree are toxic if ingested by humans.

Characteristics of Irish Yew Trees

  • Evergreen conifer with dark green, needle-like foliage
  • Narrow, upright, columnar crown
  • Produces red, berry-like arils surrounding seeds
  • Shade- and sun-tolerant, slow-growing
  • Native to Ireland; a cultivated form of English Yew

Common Uses

  • Formal gardens, hedges, and cemetery plantings
  • Ornamental columnar accent in landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat for birds
  • Privacy screens and windbreaks
  • Topiary and container planting in landscaping
