Western Yew Tree
The Western Yew Tree (Taxus brevifolia var. occidentalis) is an evergreen conifer native to western North America. It is closely related to the Pacific Yew and valued for its dense foliage and use in natural landscaping.

Western Yew trees typically grow 8–15 meters tall with a narrow, spreading crown. They thrive in shaded forest understories, often under large conifers, and prefer moist, well-drained soils. The bright red arils feed birds, while the bark and leaves contain toxic compounds.

Characteristics of Western Yew Trees

  • Evergreen conifer with dark green, needle-like foliage
  • Narrow, spreading crown, small to medium-sized tree
  • Produces red, berry-like arils surrounding seeds
  • Shade-tolerant and prefers moist forest soils
  • Native to western North America

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in shaded landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat for forest birds
  • Conservation of forest understory ecosystems
  • Specialty wood for small craftwork
  • Traditional medicinal applications
