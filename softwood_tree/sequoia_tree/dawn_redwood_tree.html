Dawn Redwood Tree
The Dawn Redwood (Metasequoia glyptostroboides) is a deciduous conifer once thought to be extinct and known only from fossils. It was rediscovered in China in the 1940s and is now widely planted around the world.

Dawn Redwood trees can grow 30–50 meters tall and have a tall, straight trunk with a pyramidal crown. Unlike Coast Redwoods and Giant Sequoias, this species is deciduous and sheds its soft feathery needles each autumn after turning a warm orange-brown color.

The species grows well in moist soils and temperate climates and is often planted as an ornamental tree in parks, botanical gardens, and large landscapes.

Characteristics of Dawn Redwood Trees

  • Deciduous conifer that sheds needles in autumn
  • Soft, feathery bright green foliage
  • Leaves turn orange-brown before falling
  • Fast-growing tree with pyramidal shape
  • Native to central China

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in parks and large gardens
  • Botanical gardens and educational landscapes
  • Shade tree in large open areas
  • Conservation plantings and reforestation
  • Wildlife habitat in landscaped environments
