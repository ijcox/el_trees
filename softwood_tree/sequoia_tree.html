Sequoia Tree
Sequoia trees are massive evergreen conifers of the genus Sequoiadendron and Sequoia. They are famous for being some of the largest and oldest living trees on Earth, primarily native to California.

Sequoias thrive in temperate forests with sufficient rainfall and are known for their thick reddish-brown bark, conical crowns, and longevity. They provide iconic forest landscapes, ecological habitats, and are a key focus of conservation efforts.

Characteristics of Sequoia Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like foliage
  • Among the largest trees by volume in the world
  • Extremely long-lived, some over 3,000 years
  • Thick, fire-resistant bark and conical crown shape
  • Provide critical habitat for forest wildlife

Common Uses

  • Timber (historically) for construction and specialized uses
  • Ornamental planting and forest conservation
  • Tourism and natural heritage education
  • Ecological support for plants and wildlife
  • Carbon sequestration and climate regulation
