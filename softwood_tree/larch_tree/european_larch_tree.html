European Larch Tree
The European Larch (Larix decidua) is a deciduous conifer native to the mountains of central Europe. Unlike most conifers, it sheds its needles in autumn, turning a brilliant golden color before dropping.

European Larch trees can grow up to 30–45 meters tall with a straight trunk and open crown. They thrive in cool mountain climates and well-drained soils. The tree produces small upright cones and soft, bright green needles that grow in clusters along short shoots.

Characteristics of European Larch Trees

  • Deciduous conifer that sheds needles in autumn
  • Soft, bright green needles growing in clusters
  • Needles turn golden yellow before falling
  • Small upright cones
  • Native to mountainous regions of central Europe

Common Uses

  • Durable timber for construction and outdoor structures
  • Boat building and fencing
  • Ornamental planting in parks and landscapes
  • Reforestation and erosion control in mountainous areas
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and forest animals
