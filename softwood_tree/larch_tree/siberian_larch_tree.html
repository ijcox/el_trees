Siberian Larch Tree
The Siberian Larch (Larix sibirica) is a hardy deciduous conifer native to the vast forests of Siberia and northern Asia. It is well known for its extreme cold tolerance and its golden autumn foliage.

Siberian Larch trees grow between 20 and 40 meters tall and develop a straight trunk with a pyramidal crown. Their soft green needles grow in clusters and turn bright yellow in autumn before falling. The species forms extensive forests across northern Russia and is one of the dominant trees of the boreal biome.

Characteristics of Siberian Larch Trees

  • Deciduous conifer that sheds needles each autumn
  • Soft light-green needles growing in clusters
  • Needles turn golden yellow before falling
  • Highly tolerant of cold climates
  • Native to Siberia and northern Asia

Common Uses

  • Durable timber for construction and outdoor structures
  • Flooring, decking, and cladding
  • Reforestation in cold northern regions
  • Wildlife habitat in boreal forests
  • Ornamental planting in large landscapes
