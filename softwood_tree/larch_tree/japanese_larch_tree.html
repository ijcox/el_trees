Japanese Larch Tree
The Japanese Larch (Larix kaempferi) is a deciduous conifer native to the mountainous regions of Japan. It is admired for its soft blue-green needles and its golden autumn foliage.

Japanese Larch trees typically grow between 20 and 40 meters tall with a broad, conical crown. Their soft needles grow in clusters and turn bright yellow in autumn before falling. The species is valued for its adaptability and is widely planted in forestry plantations outside its native range.

Characteristics of Japanese Larch Trees

  • Deciduous conifer with soft blue-green needles
  • Needles grow in dense clusters along short shoots
  • Foliage turns bright yellow before dropping in autumn
  • Produces small upright cones with distinctive scales
  • Native to the mountains of Japan

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction and outdoor structures
  • Forestry plantations and reforestation projects
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Windbreaks and soil stabilization
  • Wildlife habitat in forest ecosystems
