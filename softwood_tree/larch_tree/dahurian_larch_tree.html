Dahurian Larch Tree
The Dahurian Larch (Larix gmelinii) is a deciduous conifer native to northeastern Asia, including Siberia, Mongolia, and parts of northern China. It is known for its exceptional cold tolerance and ability to grow in harsh northern climates.

Dahurian Larch trees typically grow 20–35 meters tall with a narrow, conical crown. They thrive in extremely cold environments and are often found growing on permafrost soils. Like other larch species, their soft green needles grow in clusters and turn bright yellow before falling in autumn.

Characteristics of Dahurian Larch Trees

  • Deciduous conifer with soft clustered needles
  • Needles turn bright yellow before falling in autumn
  • Small upright cones
  • Extremely cold-hardy and tolerant of permafrost soils
  • Native to northeastern Asia and Siberia

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction and outdoor structures
  • Reforestation in cold and northern regions
  • Soil stabilization in harsh environments
  • Wildlife habitat in boreal forests
  • Research on cold-climate forest ecosystems
