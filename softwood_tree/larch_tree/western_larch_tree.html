Western Larch Tree
The Western Larch (Larix occidentalis) is a large deciduous conifer native to the mountains of western North America. It is known for its tall straight trunk, soft needles, and brilliant golden color in autumn.

Western Larch trees can reach heights of 50–60 meters, making them one of the tallest larch species. They grow in mountainous forests of the Pacific Northwest and inland regions of the United States and Canada. Their bright green needles grow in clusters and turn vivid yellow before falling in autumn.

Characteristics of Western Larch Trees

  • Deciduous conifer that sheds needles in autumn
  • Soft, bright green needles growing in clusters
  • Needles turn golden yellow before falling
  • Tall, straight trunk with open crown
  • Native to western North America

Common Uses

  • Durable timber for construction and outdoor structures
  • Railroad ties, poles, and heavy timber
  • Reforestation and forest management
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
  • Ornamental planting in large landscapes and parks
