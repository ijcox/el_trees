Juniper Tree
Juniper trees are coniferous trees and shrubs of the genus Juniperus. They are widely distributed across the Northern Hemisphere and are known for their aromatic foliage and berry-like cones.

Junipers thrive in temperate and arid regions, often on rocky soils and slopes. They provide essential habitat for wildlife, and some species are cultivated for ornamental purposes, timber, and flavoring in culinary and alcoholic beverages.

Characteristics of Juniper Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like or scale-like leaves
  • Produce berry-like cones used in culinary and medicinal applications
  • Adapted to temperate, semi-arid, and rocky environments
  • Often low-growing shrubs or medium-sized trees
  • Provide food and shelter for wildlife

Common Uses

  • Timber for fence posts, furniture, and specialty woodwork
  • Ornamental landscaping and bonsai cultivation
  • Flavoring for gin and culinary spices (juniper berries)
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological restoration
  • Essential oils and aromatic wood products
