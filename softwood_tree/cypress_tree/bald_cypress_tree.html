Bald Cypress Tree
The Bald Cypress Tree (Taxodium distichum) is a deciduous conifer native to the southeastern United States. It is well known for thriving in wetlands and for its distinctive "knees" that rise above the water around its roots.

Bald Cypress trees can grow 30–40 meters tall and are commonly found in swamps, riverbanks, and floodplains. Unlike most conifers, they lose their soft needles in autumn, which turn a reddish-brown color before falling.

Characteristics of Bald Cypress Trees

  • Deciduous conifer that sheds needles in autumn
  • Soft feathery foliage that turns reddish-brown in fall
  • Develops distinctive root "knees" in wet environments
  • Highly tolerant of flooding and swamp conditions
  • Native to the southeastern United States

Common Uses

  • Landscaping and ornamental planting
  • Timber for durable outdoor construction
  • Wetland restoration projects
  • Wildlife habitat in swamp ecosystems
  • Shade tree for parks and large landscapes
