Tsuga Heterophylla Tree
Tsuga Heterophylla, commonly known as the Western Hemlock, is a prominent conifer native to the Pacific Northwest of North America. It features soft, feathery needles and a tall, straight trunk.

Tsuga Heterophylla can grow up to 70–80 meters tall with a narrow, conical crown. It produces small, hanging cones and thrives in moist, shaded forests. This species is often a dominant tree in temperate rainforests along the western coast of North America.

Characteristics of Tsuga Heterophylla Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with soft, feathery dark green needles
  • Tall, straight trunk with narrow conical crown
  • Produce small, pendant cones
  • Native to western North America
  • Shade-tolerant and long-lived

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, plywood, and paper products
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large gardens
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
  • Forest restoration and erosion control
  • Traditional uses by indigenous peoples for various purposes
