Eastern Hemlock Tree
The Eastern Hemlock (Tsuga canadensis) is a large, shade-tolerant conifer native to eastern North America. It is known for its graceful, drooping branches and fine, dark green needles.

Eastern Hemlocks grow up to 30 meters tall with a dense, pyramidal crown. The tree produces small, pendant cones and thrives in moist, well-drained soils. It is commonly found in forests along streams and cool, shaded slopes.

Characteristics of Eastern Hemlock Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with fine, dark green needles
  • Graceful, drooping branches forming a pyramidal crown
  • Produce small, pendant cones
  • Native to eastern North America
  • Shade-tolerant and long-lived

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in parks and gardens
  • Timber for construction and furniture
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
  • Shade trees in large landscapes and forests
  • Erosion control on slopes and near streams
