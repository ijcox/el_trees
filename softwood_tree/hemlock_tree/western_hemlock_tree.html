Western Hemlock Tree
The Western Hemlock (Tsuga heterophylla) is a large conifer native to the Pacific Northwest of North America. It is recognized for its tall, straight trunk and feathery, dark green foliage.

Western Hemlocks can reach heights of 70–80 meters with a narrow, conical crown. The tree produces small, hanging cones and thrives in moist, shaded forests, often along streams and in coastal regions. It is a dominant species in temperate rainforests of western North America.

Characteristics of Western Hemlock Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with soft, feathery needles
  • Tall, straight trunk with narrow conical crown
  • Produce small, pendant cones
  • Native to western North America
  • Shade-tolerant and long-lived

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, plywood, and paper products
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large gardens
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
  • Forest restoration and erosion control
  • Traditional uses by indigenous peoples for various purposes
