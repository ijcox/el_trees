Southern Japanese Hemlock Tree
The Southern Japanese Hemlock (Tsuga sieboldii) is a conifer native to southern Japan. It is known for its elegant, drooping branches and dark green, glossy needles.

Southern Japanese Hemlocks grow 20–30 meters tall with a slender, pyramidal crown. They produce small, hanging cones and thrive in cool, humid forests. This species is often used in traditional Japanese gardens and bonsai cultivation.

Characteristics of Southern Japanese Hemlock Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with dark green, glossy needles
  • Slender, pyramidal crown with drooping branches
  • Produce small, pendant cones
  • Native to southern Japan
  • Shade-tolerant and long-lived

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens and parks
  • Bonsai cultivation
  • Timber for light construction
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
  • Cultural and landscape uses in traditional Japanese gardens
