Mountain Hemlock Tree
The Mountain Hemlock (Tsuga mertensiana) is a high-altitude conifer native to the mountains of western North America. It is known for its slender, conical shape and soft, blue-green needles.

Mountain Hemlocks typically grow 20–40 meters tall, with a narrow, pyramidal crown. The tree produces small, pendant cones and thrives in cold, moist, subalpine environments. It often forms dense forests near the treeline and contributes to alpine biodiversity.

Characteristics of Mountain Hemlock Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with soft, blue-green needles
  • Slender, conical crown and straight trunk
  • Produce small, hanging cones
  • Native to high-altitude regions of western North America
  • Cold-tolerant and long-lived

Common Uses

  • Timber for light construction and pulp
  • Ornamental planting in mountain and cold-climate gardens
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
  • Subalpine forest restoration and erosion control
  • Research on high-altitude ecosystems and climate adaptation
