Carolina Hemlock Tree
The Carolina Hemlock (Tsuga caroliniana) is a rare conifer native to the southern Appalachian Mountains of the United States. It is distinguished by its short, dark green needles and small, drooping cones.

Carolina Hemlocks typically grow 15–25 meters tall with a narrow, conical crown. They prefer steep, rocky slopes and well-drained soils in high-elevation forests. This species is considered endangered due to threats from the hemlock woolly adelgid and habitat loss.

Characteristics of Carolina Hemlock Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with short, dark green needles
  • Narrow, conical crown with a straight trunk
  • Produce small, pendant cones
  • Native to the southern Appalachian Mountains
  • Shade-tolerant but slow-growing

Common Uses

  • Conservation and habitat restoration
  • Ornamental planting in botanical gardens
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
  • Research on endangered tree species and pest resistance
  • Educational programs in forest ecology
