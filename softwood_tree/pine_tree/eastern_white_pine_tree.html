Eastern White Pine Tree
The Eastern White Pine (Pinus strobus) is a tall, fast-growing evergreen native to eastern North America. It is prized for its soft, straight timber and long, flexible needles.

Eastern White Pines can reach heights of 50–70 meters, making them one of the tallest trees in eastern North America. They have long, slender needles in bundles of five and produce elongated, cylindrical cones. These trees are commonly used in timber production, landscaping, and reforestation.

Characteristics of Eastern White Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in bundles of five (7–13 cm long)
  • Bark is gray-brown and deeply furrowed with age
  • Produce cylindrical cones 8–20 cm long that release winged seeds
  • Fast-growing and tolerant of a range of soils, including sandy and loamy soils
  • Lifespan typically 200–400 years

Common Uses

  • Softwood timber for construction, furniture, and cabinetry
  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Ornamental planting and landscaping
  • Windbreaks and reforestation projects
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and small mammals
