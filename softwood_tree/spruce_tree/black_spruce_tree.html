Black Spruce Tree
The Black Spruce (Picea mariana) is a small to medium evergreen native to northern North America. It thrives in cold climates and is commonly found in boreal forests and wetland areas.

Black Spruces typically grow 10–20 meters tall with a narrow, conical crown. They have short, stiff, blue-green needles and produce small cones. This species is highly adapted to poor, acidic, and waterlogged soils and is important for pulpwood and wildlife habitat.

Characteristics of Black Spruce Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with short, stiff, blue-green needles (0.7–1.8 cm long)
  • Bark is thin, scaly, and gray-brown
  • Produce small cones 1.5–3 cm long
  • Adapted to cold, wet, acidic, or sandy soils in boreal forests
  • Lifespan typically 200–300 years

Common Uses

  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Reforestation and erosion control in boreal forests
  • Ornamental planting in cold climates
  • Wildlife habitat for birds, small mammals, and moose
  • Christmas tree cultivation
