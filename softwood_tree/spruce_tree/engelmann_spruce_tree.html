Engelmann Spruce Tree
The Engelmann Spruce (Picea engelmannii) is a medium to large evergreen native to the Rocky Mountains and western North America. It is valued for its timber, paper pulp, and use in reforestation.

Engelmann Spruces typically grow 20–40 meters tall with a narrow, conical crown. They have short, blue-green needles and produce slender, cylindrical cones. This species thrives at high elevations in cool, moist climates and is commonly used in forestry and ornamental planting.

Characteristics of Engelmann Spruce Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with short, stiff, blue-green needles (1.5–3 cm long)
  • Bark is thin, scaly, and gray-brown
  • Produce slender cones 4–9 cm long
  • Adapted to high-elevation, cool, and moist environments
  • Lifespan typically 300–400 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, furniture, and paper pulp
  • Reforestation and erosion control projects
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
  • Christmas tree cultivation at higher elevations
