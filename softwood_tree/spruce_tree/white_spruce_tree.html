White Spruce Tree
The White Spruce (Picea glauca) is a medium to large evergreen native to northern North America. It is valued for timber, pulpwood, and its use in ornamental and Christmas tree plantings.

White Spruces typically grow 20–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and narrow, conical crown. They have short, stiff needles that are blue-green, and produce slender cones. This species is highly adaptable to cold climates and is commonly used in forestry and landscaping.

Characteristics of White Spruce Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with short, stiff, blue-green needles (1–2.5 cm long)
  • Bark is thin and scaly, gray to reddish-brown
  • Produce slender cones 3–7 cm long
  • Adapted to cold climates and a variety of soils, including sandy and loamy
  • Lifespan typically 200–300 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, furniture, and pulpwood
  • Christmas tree plantations and ornamental planting
  • Windbreaks and reforestation projects
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
  • Soil stabilization and erosion control
