Japanese Chestnut Tree
The Japanese Chestnut Tree (Castanea crenata) is native to Japan and Korea. It is known for its resistance to chestnut blight and for producing sweet, flavorful nuts.

Japanese Chestnut trees grow 10–20 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They prefer well-drained soils and full sunlight. Leaves are simple, elongated, and serrated. The tree produces spiny husks containing sweet, edible chestnuts and is widely cultivated for nut production.

Characteristics of Japanese Chestnut Trees

  • Simple, elongated, serrated leaves
  • Broad, rounded crown
  • Disease-resistant, especially to chestnut blight
  • Produces sweet edible nuts in spiny husks
  • Thrives in well-drained soils and full sun
  • Native to Japan and Korea

Common Uses

  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Ornamental planting and shade tree
  • Timber for light construction and furniture
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for mammals and birds
  • Hybrid breeding programs for disease resistance
