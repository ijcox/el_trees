American Chestnut Tree
The American Chestnut Tree (Castanea dentata) was once a dominant tree in eastern North American forests. It is known for its rapid growth, tall straight trunk, and edible nuts.

American Chestnut trees can grow 20–30 meters tall with a straight, towering trunk and rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained, acidic soils with full sunlight. Leaves are simple, elongated, and serrated. Unfortunately, most populations were devastated by chestnut blight in the 20th century.

Characteristics of American Chestnut Trees

  • Large, simple, serrated leaves
  • Straight, tall trunk with rounded crown
  • Rapid growth and formerly long-lived
  • Produces edible nuts in spiny husks
  • Thrives in well-drained, acidic soils
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, cabinetry, and flooring
  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Ornamental planting and shade tree
  • Wildlife food source, especially for deer and birds
  • Soil stabilization and forest regeneration
