White Willow Tree
The White Willow Tree (Salix alba) is a fast-growing deciduous tree native to Europe and western Asia. It is known for its slender leaves, flexible branches, and tolerance to wet conditions.

White Willow trees grow 15–25 meters tall with a broad, open crown. They thrive in moist soils and are commonly found near rivers, lakes, and wetlands. The leaves are narrow and silvery-green, and the tree produces small catkins in spring.

Characteristics of White Willow Trees

  • Narrow, silvery-green leaves
  • Broad, open crown with flexible branches
  • Produces catkins in spring
  • Thrives in wet and flood-prone soils
  • Fast-growing and short to medium lifespan
  • Native to Europe and western Asia

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting near water features
  • Soil stabilization along riverbanks
  • Source of salicin (used in medicine)
  • Wood for baskets, tools, and crafts
  • Wildlife habitat
