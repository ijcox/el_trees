Purple Willow Tree
The Purple Willow Tree (Salix purpurea) is a deciduous shrub or small tree native to Europe and western Asia. It is known for its striking purple-tinged stems and slender leaves, often used in basket weaving.

Purple Willow trees grow 3–6 meters tall with a spreading, arching crown. They thrive in moist soils along riverbanks, wetlands, and floodplains. The leaves are narrow and green, while the young stems are reddish-purple, producing small catkins in spring.

Characteristics of Purple Willow Trees

  • Narrow green leaves
  • Spreading, arching crown
  • Reddish-purple young stems
  • Produces catkins in spring
  • Thrives in moist and wet soils
  • Native to Europe and western Asia

Common Uses

  • Basket weaving and crafts from flexible stems
  • Ornamental planting along water features
  • Soil stabilization along riverbanks
  • Wildlife habitat
  • Hedging and small-scale landscaping
