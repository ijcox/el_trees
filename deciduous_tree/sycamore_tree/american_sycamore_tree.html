American Sycamore Tree
The American Sycamore Tree (Platanus occidentalis) is a large deciduous tree native to eastern and central North America. It is known for its massive size, distinctive mottled bark, and broad canopy.

American Sycamore trees grow 30–40 meters tall with a wide, spreading crown. They thrive in moist, well-drained soils and are commonly found along rivers and floodplains. The leaves are large and lobed, and the bark peels off in patches revealing a smooth, whitish trunk underneath.

Characteristics of American Sycamore Trees

  • Large, lobed leaves
  • Massive tree with wide, spreading crown
  • Mottled bark peeling to reveal whitish trunk
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Thrives in moist soils, especially near rivers
  • Native to eastern and central North America

Common Uses

  • Shade tree in parks and large landscapes
  • Timber for furniture and veneer
  • Ornamental planting for its dramatic appearance
  • Wildlife habitat and erosion control along waterways
  • Windbreak and floodplain stabilization
