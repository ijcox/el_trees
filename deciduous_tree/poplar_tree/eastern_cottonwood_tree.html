Eastern Cottonwood Tree
The Eastern Cottonwood Tree (Populus deltoides) is a large deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is known for its rapid growth, triangular leaves, and cotton-like seed dispersal.

Eastern Cottonwood trees grow 20–35 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in moist soils, especially along rivers and floodplains. The leaves are large, triangular, and serrated, while the bark is thick, deeply furrowed, and grayish-brown.

Characteristics of Eastern Cottonwood Trees

  • Large, triangular, serrated leaves
  • Broad, rounded crown
  • Rapid growth and medium lifespan
  • Thick, furrowed gray-brown bark
  • Thrives in moist soils and floodplains
  • Produces cotton-like seeds in spring

Common Uses

  • Shade and ornamental planting along rivers and parks
  • Timber for pulp, crates, and light construction
  • Soil stabilization along waterways
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Windbreaks and erosion control
