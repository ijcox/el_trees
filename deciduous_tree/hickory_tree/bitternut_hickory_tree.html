Bitternut Hickory Tree
The Bitternut Hickory Tree (Carya cordiformis) is a medium to large deciduous tree known for its bitter nuts and straight trunk. It is native to eastern North America and commonly found in forests and along streams.

Bitternut Hickory trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a straight trunk and narrow to rounded crown. They prefer well-drained soils and full sunlight. The leaves are pinnately compound with 7 leaflets, and the nuts are small, thin-shelled, and very bitter.

Characteristics of Bitternut Hickory Trees

  • Pinnately compound leaves with 7 leaflets
  • Straight trunk with narrow to rounded crown
  • Produces small, bitter nuts
  • Hard, dense wood
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for tool handles, furniture, and flooring
  • Fuelwood and smoking wood for cooking
  • Shade and ornamental tree in parks and forests
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Natural reforestation in upland forests
