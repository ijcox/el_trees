Shellbark Hickory Tree
The Shellbark Hickory Tree (Carya laciniosa) is a large deciduous tree known for its thick, shaggy bark and sweet, edible nuts. It is native to eastern North America and typically found in rich, bottomland soils.

Shellbark Hickory trees grow 25–35 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They prefer fertile, well-drained soils and full sunlight. The leaves are pinnately compound with 5–7 leaflets, and the nuts are large, sweet, and enclosed in thick husks.

Characteristics of Shellbark Hickory Trees

  • Pinnately compound leaves with 5–7 leaflets
  • Thick, shaggy bark
  • Broad, rounded crown with straight trunk
  • Produces large, sweet nuts in thick husks
  • Moderate growth rate, long-lived
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for tool handles, furniture, and flooring
  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Shade and ornamental tree in parks and large gardens
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Firewood and smoking wood for cooking
