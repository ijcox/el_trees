Black Cottonwood Tree
The Black Cottonwood Tree (Populus trichocarpa) is a large deciduous tree native to western North America. It is the tallest poplar species in North America, recognized for its rapid growth and dark, deeply furrowed bark.

Black Cottonwood trees grow 30–50 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in moist soils along streams and rivers. The leaves are large, triangular, and serrated, and the bark is dark brown to black, thick, and deeply furrowed.

Characteristics of Black Cottonwood Trees

  • Large, triangular, serrated leaves
  • Broad, rounded crown
  • Rapid growth and long lifespan
  • Dark, thick, deeply furrowed bark
  • Thrives in moist soils along rivers and streams
  • Produces cotton-like seeds in spring

Common Uses

  • Timber for pulp, plywood, and crates
  • Shade and ornamental planting along waterways
  • Soil stabilization and erosion control
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Windbreaks and riparian buffers
