Hybrid Cottonwood Tree
The Hybrid Cottonwood Tree (Populus × acuminata) is a naturally occurring cross between the Eastern Cottonwood and Plains Cottonwood. It is valued for its rapid growth, resilience, and adaptability to a variety of North American habitats.

Hybrid Cottonwood trees grow 20–35 meters tall with a broad, spreading crown. They prefer moist soils but are tolerant of some drought conditions. The leaves are triangular to deltoid, serrated, and slightly glossy, while the bark is gray-brown and deeply furrowed.

Characteristics of Hybrid Cottonwood Trees

  • Triangular to deltoid, serrated leaves
  • Broad, spreading crown
  • Rapid growth and medium lifespan
  • Gray-brown, deeply furrowed bark
  • Tolerant of varying soil moisture and some drought
  • Hybrid of Eastern and Plains Cottonwood

Common Uses

  • Shade and ornamental planting in parks and urban areas
  • Timber for pulp, crates, and light construction
  • Soil stabilization along waterways
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Windbreaks and erosion control
