Fremont Cottonwood Tree
The Fremont Cottonwood Tree (Populus fremontii) is a large deciduous tree native to the southwestern United States and northern Mexico. It is well-adapted to riparian habitats and valued for its shade and rapid growth.

Fremont Cottonwood trees grow 15–30 meters tall with a broad, spreading crown. They thrive in moist soils along rivers, streams, and floodplains. The leaves are triangular to deltoid with serrated edges, and the bark is thick, gray to brown, and deeply furrowed.

Characteristics of Fremont Cottonwood Trees

  • Triangular to deltoid, serrated leaves
  • Broad, spreading crown
  • Rapid growth and medium lifespan
  • Thick, furrowed gray-brown bark
  • Thrives in riparian zones and floodplains
  • Produces cotton-like seeds in spring

Common Uses

  • Shade and ornamental planting along rivers and parks
  • Timber for pulp, crates, and light construction
  • Soil stabilization along waterways
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Windbreaks and riparian buffer zones
