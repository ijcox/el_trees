Plains Cottonwood Tree
The Plains Cottonwood Tree (Populus deltoides subsp. monilifera) is a subspecies of the Eastern Cottonwood, native to the central plains of North America. It is recognized for its rapid growth, triangular leaves, and tolerance to dry plains conditions.

Plains Cottonwood trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a broad, spreading crown. They thrive in moist soils along rivers but are more drought-tolerant than the Eastern Cottonwood. The leaves are large, triangular, and serrated, and the bark is thick, furrowed, and grayish-brown.

Characteristics of Plains Cottonwood Trees

  • Large, triangular, serrated leaves
  • Broad, spreading crown
  • Rapid growth and medium lifespan
  • Thick, furrowed gray-brown bark
  • More drought-tolerant than Eastern Cottonwood
  • Produces cotton-like seeds in spring

Common Uses

  • Shade and ornamental planting in plains regions
  • Timber for pulp, crates, and light construction
  • Soil stabilization along rivers and floodplains
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Windbreaks and erosion control
