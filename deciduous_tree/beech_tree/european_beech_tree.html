European Beech Tree
The European Beech Tree (Fagus sylvatica) is a large deciduous tree known for its smooth gray bark and dense, spreading canopy. It is native to Europe and widely planted as an ornamental and shade tree.

European Beech trees can grow 30–40 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and temperate climates. Their dense foliage creates deep shade, and their smooth bark remains intact even as the tree ages.

Characteristics of European Beech Trees

  • Smooth, light gray bark
  • Broad, dense canopy providing deep shade
  • Oval leaves with slightly wavy edges
  • Produces small edible nuts called beechnuts
  • Long-lived and slow to moderate growth
  • Native to Europe

Common Uses

  • Ornamental and shade tree in parks and gardens
  • Timber for furniture and flooring
  • Hedges and landscaping features
  • Wildlife food source (beechnuts)
  • Firewood and charcoal production
