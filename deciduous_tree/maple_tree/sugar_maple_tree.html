Sugar Maple Tree
The Sugar Maple Tree (Acer saccharum) is a large deciduous tree native to eastern North America. It is renowned for its sweet sap used to produce maple syrup and its vibrant fall foliage.

Sugar Maple trees grow 20–30 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sun, commonly found in forests, parks, and large landscapes. The sap is harvested for maple syrup, and the wood is valued for furniture, flooring, and cabinetry.

Characteristics of Sugar Maple Trees

  • Deciduous tree with broad, lobed green leaves turning orange, red, and yellow in fall
  • Rounded crown with strong, durable wood
  • Produces sap for maple syrup
  • Long-lived and slow-growing
  • Native to eastern North America

Common Uses

  • Maple syrup production from sap
  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and cabinetry
  • Ornamental planting in parks and landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and mammals
  • Shade tree in large landscapes
