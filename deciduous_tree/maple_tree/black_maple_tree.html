Black Maple Tree
The Black Maple Tree (Acer nigrum) is a deciduous tree native to the midwestern and eastern United States. It is closely related to the Sugar Maple, recognized for its dark bark and maple syrup potential.

Black Maple trees grow 15–25 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sun to partial shade. The leaves turn golden-yellow in fall, and the sap can be used for syrup production. The wood is valued for furniture, flooring, and cabinetry.

Characteristics of Black Maple Trees

  • Deciduous tree with three-lobed green leaves turning yellow in fall
  • Broad, rounded crown with dark, furrowed bark
  • Produces sap suitable for maple syrup
  • Moderate growth rate and long-lived
  • Native to midwestern and eastern United States

Common Uses

  • Maple syrup production from sap
  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and cabinetry
  • Ornamental planting in parks and landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and acorn food source
  • Shade tree in open areas
