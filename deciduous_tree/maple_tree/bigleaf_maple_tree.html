Bigleaf Maple Tree
The Bigleaf Maple Tree (Acer macrophyllum) is a large deciduous tree native to western North America. It is notable for having some of the largest leaves of any maple species and a broad, spreading canopy.

Bigleaf Maple trees grow 20–35 meters tall with a wide, spreading crown. They thrive in moist, well-drained soils and full sun to partial shade. The large leaves create dense shade, and the tree produces winged seeds called samaras. The wood is used for furniture, veneers, and specialty woodworking.

Characteristics of Bigleaf Maple Trees

  • Deciduous tree with very large, five-lobed green leaves turning yellow in fall
  • Wide, spreading crown providing dense shade
  • Produces winged seeds called samaras
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Native to western North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, veneers, and specialty woodworking
  • Ornamental planting in parks and landscapes
  • Shade tree in large gardens and open areas
  • Wildlife habitat and seed food source
  • Riparian and erosion control planting
