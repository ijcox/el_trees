Cottonwood Tree
Cottonwood trees are fast-growing deciduous trees of the genus Populus, closely related to poplars. They are native to North America and are recognized for their broad leaves, cotton-like seed tufts, and rapid growth.

Cottonwoods thrive in moist soils, often along rivers, lakes, and floodplains. They are commonly used for shade, erosion control, timber, and ornamental planting.

Characteristics of Cottonwood Trees

  • Deciduous trees with broad, triangular leaves
  • Rapid growth and short to moderate lifespan
  • Produce cotton-like tufts surrounding seeds in spring
  • Adapted to moist soils and riparian zones
  • Provide shade, wildlife habitat, and bank stabilization

Common Uses

  • Shade trees in parks, streets, and landscapes
  • Erosion control along riverbanks and wetlands
  • Timber for paper, light construction, and wood products
  • Ornamental planting and windbreaks
  • Wildlife habitat for birds, insects, and small mammals
