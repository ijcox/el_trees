Yellow Birch Tree
The Yellow Birch Tree (Betula alleghaniensis) is a large deciduous tree known for its yellowish-bronze peeling bark and strong, valuable wood. It is native to northeastern North America and commonly found in cool, moist forests.

Yellow Birch trees can grow 18–30 meters tall with a broad crown and sturdy trunk. They prefer rich, moist, well-drained soils and are often found in mixed hardwood forests. The bark peels in thin, curly strips and has a distinctive wintergreen scent when scratched.

Characteristics of Yellow Birch Trees

  • Yellow to bronze peeling bark with curly strips
  • Large deciduous tree with broad crown
  • Leaves are oval with finely toothed edges
  • Strong, heavy wood
  • Prefers cool, moist forest environments
  • Native to northeastern North America

Common Uses

  • High-quality timber for furniture and flooring
  • Veneer and cabinetry production
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Ornamental planting in large landscapes
  • Traditional uses for extracts and oils
