Paper Birch Tree
The Paper Birch Tree (Betula papyrifera) is a deciduous tree famous for its bright white, papery bark that peels in thin layers. It is native to northern North America and thrives in cool climates.

Paper Birch trees typically grow 15–20 meters tall with a slender trunk and rounded crown. They prefer well-drained soils and cooler temperatures, often found in forests and near lakes. The bark is highly distinctive and has historically been used for making canoes and containers.

Characteristics of Paper Birch Trees

  • Bright white bark that peels in thin, papery sheets
  • Medium-sized deciduous tree
  • Oval leaves with serrated edges
  • Moderate growth rate
  • Prefers cool climates and well-drained soil
  • Native to northern North America

Common Uses

  • Ornamental landscaping tree
  • Wood used for pulp and paper
  • Traditional use for canoes, baskets, and shelters
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Decorative bark for crafts
