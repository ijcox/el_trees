Black Oak Tree
The Black Oak Tree (Quercus velutina) is a large deciduous tree native to eastern and central North America. It is recognized for its dark bark, lobed leaves, and high-quality timber.

Black Oak trees typically grow 20–30 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They prefer well-drained soils and full sun and are often found in forests, parks, and large landscapes. The acorns provide food for wildlife, while the wood is used for furniture, flooring, and construction.

Characteristics of Black Oak Trees

  • Deciduous tree with lobed green leaves turning reddish-brown in fall
  • Broad, rounded crown with dark, furrowed bark
  • Produces acorns that feed wildlife
  • Moderate to fast-growing and long-lived
  • Native to eastern and central North America

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and construction
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat and acorn food source
  • Shade tree in open areas
  • Forest restoration and erosion control
