Sycamore Tree
Sycamore trees are large deciduous trees belonging to the genus Platanus. They are native to temperate regions of North America, Europe, and Asia, and are known for their broad leaves and distinctive exfoliating bark.

Sycamores thrive in fertile, moist soils and are often found along riverbanks and floodplains. They are valued for shade, timber, and ornamental purposes in parks and urban landscapes.

Characteristics of Sycamore Trees

  • Deciduous trees with large, broad leaves
  • Distinctive exfoliating bark revealing a patchwork of colors
  • Fast to moderate growth rate with wide-spreading crown
  • Adapted to temperate climates and fertile, moist soils
  • Provide shade, habitat, and aesthetic value in landscapes

Common Uses

  • Shade and ornamental trees in parks, streets, and gardens
  • Timber for furniture, veneer, and interior woodwork
  • Riparian and floodplain stabilization
  • Wildlife habitat for birds, insects, and small mammals
  • Urban landscaping due to pollution tolerance
