Black Ash Tree
The Black Ash Tree (Fraxinus nigra) is a medium-sized deciduous tree known for its pliable wood and tolerance of wet soils. It is native to northeastern North America and commonly found in swamps and along riverbanks.

Black Ash trees typically grow 15–20 meters tall with a straight trunk and rounded crown. They thrive in wet, poorly drained soils and are often used in basket weaving due to their easily split wood. The leaves are compound with 7–11 leaflets, turning yellow in autumn.

Characteristics of Black Ash Trees

  • Compound leaves with 7–11 leaflets
  • Straight trunk with rounded crown
  • Tolerates wet and poorly drained soils
  • Wood is flexible and easy to split
  • Moderate growth rate
  • Native to northeastern North America

Common Uses

  • Basket weaving and traditional crafts
  • Timber for furniture and flooring
  • Shade tree in wetland areas
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Ornamental planting in parks
