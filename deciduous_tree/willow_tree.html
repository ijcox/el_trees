Willow Tree
Willow trees are deciduous trees and shrubs of the genus Salix. They are native to temperate and cold regions of the Northern Hemisphere and are famous for their slender, flexible branches and graceful appearance.

Willows thrive in moist soils and along riverbanks, lakes, and wetlands. They are valued for ornamental planting, erosion control, and ecological support.

Characteristics of Willow Trees

  • Deciduous trees with narrow, lance-shaped leaves
  • Slender, flexible branches often drooping or weeping
  • Fast-growing and tolerant of wet conditions
  • Adapted to temperate climates and riparian zones
  • Provide food and habitat for birds, insects, and wildlife

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, parks, and riverbanks
  • Erosion control along water bodies and wetland restoration
  • Wood for furniture, baskets, and cricket bats
  • Traditional medicinal uses (e.g., willow bark for salicin)
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological support
