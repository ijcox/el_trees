Yew Tree
Yew trees are evergreen conifers belonging to the genus Taxus. They are native to Europe, Asia, and North America, and are highly valued for their ornamental use, longevity, and toxic yet medicinal compounds.

Yews are slow-growing trees or shrubs, often with dark green needle-like foliage and red arils surrounding seeds. They are used in landscaping, hedging, and historically for producing longbows and modern medicines.

Characteristics of Yew Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with flat, dark green needles
  • Red arils (berry-like structures) surrounding seeds
  • Slow-growing and long-lived
  • Tolerant of shade and adaptable to temperate climates
  • All parts (except arils) are highly toxic but contain medicinal compounds

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting and hedges in gardens and parks
  • Historical use in crafting longbows
  • Source of paclitaxel (taxol) for cancer treatment
  • Timber for small woodcraft projects
  • Wildlife habitat, especially birds that eat the arils
