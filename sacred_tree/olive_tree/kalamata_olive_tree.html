Kalamata Olive Tree
The Kalamata Olive Tree (Olea europaea 'Kalamata') is a cultivar native to Greece, famous for its large, dark purple olives used for table consumption and olive oil.

Kalamata Olive trees grow 8–12 meters tall with a rounded, dense crown. They prefer well-drained, alkaline soils and full sunlight. Leaves are evergreen, narrow, and silvery-green, and the tree produces large, almond-shaped, dark purple olives.

Characteristics of Kalamata Olive Trees

  • Evergreen, narrow, silvery-green leaves
  • Rounded, dense crown
  • Produces large, dark purple, almond-shaped olives
  • Slow-growing, long-lived tree
  • Thrives in well-drained, alkaline soils and full sun
  • Native cultivar from Greece

Common Uses

  • Table olives, particularly in Mediterranean cuisine
  • Olive oil production
  • Ornamental planting in gardens and patios
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and insects
  • Traditional Mediterranean landscapes and culinary heritage
