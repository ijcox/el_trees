Ficus Tree
Ficus trees (Ficus spp.) are a large genus of trees, shrubs, and climbers native to tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. They are known for their aerial roots, dense foliage, and in some species, edible figs.

Ficus trees thrive in well-drained soil with ample sunlight, though some species can tolerate partial shade. They are widely used for ornamental planting, indoor decoration, and ecological restoration due to their rapid growth and canopy formation.

Common Types of Ficus Trees

Ficus Benghalensis (Banyan) Ficus Religiosa (Sacred Fig) Ficus Carica (Common Fig) Ficus Microcarpa Ficus Elastica (Rubber Plant)

Characteristics of Ficus Trees

  • Evergreen or deciduous depending on species
  • Aerial roots in many species, often forming dense canopies
  • Leaves vary: glossy, broad, or heart-shaped depending on species
  • Small flowers hidden inside figs; fruit often edible
  • Rapid growth rate; adaptable to different environments

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in gardens, streets, and indoor spaces
  • Shade trees in parks and urban areas
  • Traditional medicine and cultural significance in some species
  • Edible figs for food and wildlife
  • Soil stabilization and ecological restoration in tropical areas
