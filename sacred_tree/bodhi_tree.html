Bodhi Tree
The Bodhi Tree (Ficus religiosa) is a sacred fig tree native to the Indian subcontinent. It is revered in Buddhism as the tree under which Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment, and is considered holy in several religions.

Bodhi trees thrive in tropical and subtropical climates, preferring well-drained soil and full sunlight. They are often planted near temples, monasteries, and sacred sites due to their spiritual significance.

Characteristics of Bodhi Trees

  • Deciduous tree, typically 60–100 feet tall with wide canopy
  • Heart-shaped leaves with extended tips, glossy green turning yellow in dry season
  • Small figs as fruit, ripening to purplish-red
  • Fast-growing under ideal conditions; long-lived
  • Spreading root system, often forming aerial roots

Common Uses

  • Religious and spiritual significance in Buddhism, Hinduism, and Jainism
  • Ornamental and shade planting in parks, temples, and gardens
  • Educational and cultural heritage sites
  • Traditional medicine: leaves and bark used in some remedies
  • Environmental: provides habitat and food for birds and insects
