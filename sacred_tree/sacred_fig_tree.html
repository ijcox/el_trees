Sacred Fig Tree
The Sacred Fig Tree (Ficus religiosa), also known as the Peepal or Bodhi Tree, is a sacred species native to the Indian subcontinent. It holds great religious and cultural importance in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism.

Sacred Fig trees grow best in tropical and subtropical climates with full sunlight and well-drained soil. They are often planted at temples, sacred groves, and parks as a symbol of spirituality and longevity.

Characteristics of Sacred Fig Trees

  • Deciduous tree, 50–100 feet tall, with broad canopy
  • Heart-shaped leaves with elongated tips; turns yellow in dry season
  • Figs as fruit, small and purplish-red when ripe
  • Rapid growth and long-lived; aerial roots develop from branches
  • Strong cultural and spiritual significance

Common Uses

  • Religious and spiritual ceremonies and worship
  • Ornamental and shade planting in temples, parks, and sacred groves
  • Traditional medicine uses from leaves, bark, and figs
  • Cultural heritage and educational purposes
  • Supports local biodiversity as a habitat and food source for wildlife
