Ficus Benghalensis (Banyan)
The Banyan tree is a massive, long-living fig species known for its aerial roots and expansive canopy.

Ficus benghalensis is one of the largest tree species in the world by canopy coverage. It is native to the Indian subcontinent and is famous for its ability to spread laterally using aerial prop roots that become new trunks.

The Banyan tree is culturally significant in many regions of Asia and is often associated with longevity, stability, and sacred spaces.

Characteristics of Banyan Tree

  • Massive spreading canopy
  • Aerial prop roots forming secondary trunks
  • Evergreen fig species
  • Extremely long lifespan
  • Thrives in tropical climates
  • Provides extensive shade coverage

Common Uses

  • Shade tree in large open areas
  • Religious and cultural sites
  • Landmark and heritage tree planting
  • Ecological habitat support
  • Ornamental and park planting
