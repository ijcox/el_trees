Live Oak Tree
The Live Oak Tree (Quercus virginiana) is a large evergreen or semi-evergreen oak native to the southeastern United States. It is admired for its sprawling, picturesque branches and long lifespan.

Live Oak trees can grow 15–25 meters tall with wide, spreading branches forming a massive, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained soils and full sun, often in coastal and southern landscapes. Acorns provide food for wildlife, while the dense wood is used in construction and furniture.

Characteristics of Live Oak Trees

  • Evergreen or semi-evergreen with dark green, leathery leaves
  • Massive, spreading crown with long, picturesque branches
  • Produces acorns that feed birds and mammals
  • Slow-growing, long-lived, often over 200 years
  • Native to the southeastern United States

Common Uses

  • Ornamental planting in parks, gardens, and large landscapes
  • Timber for furniture, construction, and shipbuilding
  • Wildlife habitat and acorn food source
  • Shade tree in open areas
  • Coastal and erosion control planting
