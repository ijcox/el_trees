Beaked Hazelnut Tree
The Beaked Hazelnut Tree (Corylus cornuta) is a North American native species known for its distinctive elongated husk or “beak” that encloses the nut.

Beaked hazelnut trees grow 2–6 meters tall and often form dense thickets in woodland and forest edges. They thrive in cool temperate climates and are highly adaptable to different soil conditions. The nuts are edible but smaller and harder to extract compared to cultivated varieties.

Characteristics of Beaked Hazelnut Trees

  • Multi-stemmed shrub or small tree
  • Produces nuts enclosed in a long husk (“beak”)
  • Native to North America
  • Highly cold hardy and adaptable
  • Forms dense natural thickets
  • Wildlife-supporting species

Common Uses

  • Wild nut harvesting
  • Ecological restoration
  • Wildlife food source
  • Native landscaping
  • Hedgerow and forest edge planting
