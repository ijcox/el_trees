Cashew Tree
Cashew trees (Anacardium occidentale) are evergreen tropical trees native to northeastern Brazil. They are cultivated in tropical regions worldwide for their edible seeds (cashew nuts) and the cashew apple fruit.

Cashew trees thrive in warm tropical climates with well-drained soils and full sunlight. They are commonly grown in orchards for nut production and occasionally for ornamental purposes.

Characteristics of Cashew Trees

  • Evergreen trees with spreading branches and glossy leaves
  • Typically grow 30–40 feet tall
  • Produce small, fragrant flowers in clusters
  • Fruit is a cashew apple with a kidney-shaped nut attached externally
  • Require tropical conditions and are sensitive to frost

Common Uses

  • Consumption of cashew nuts, raw, roasted, or processed
  • Used in cooking, baking, and confections
  • Juice and pulp of cashew apple used in beverages and jams
  • Ornamental planting for shade and aesthetic value
  • Commercial orchards for nut production and export
