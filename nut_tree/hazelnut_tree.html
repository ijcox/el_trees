Hazelnut Tree
Hazelnut trees (Corylus species), also known as filbert trees, are deciduous trees or large shrubs native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. They are valued for their edible nuts, ornamental foliage, and wildlife support.

Hazelnut trees grow best in temperate climates with well-drained soil and full to partial sunlight. They are commonly cultivated in orchards, gardens, and naturalized areas for nut production and ecological benefits.

Characteristics of Hazelnut Trees

  • Deciduous trees or shrubs with rounded crowns
  • Produce catkins (male flowers) in early spring and small female flowers
  • Fruit is a hard-shelled nut enclosed in a husk
  • Height ranges from 10–20 feet for shrubs and up to 40 feet for tree forms
  • Adaptable to various soils and climates within temperate regions

Common Uses

  • Consumption of raw, roasted, or processed hazelnuts
  • Used in confectionery, baking, and spreads like praline and nut butters
  • Ornamental planting due to attractive foliage and catkins
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and mammals
  • Wood used for small crafts and stakes in horticulture
