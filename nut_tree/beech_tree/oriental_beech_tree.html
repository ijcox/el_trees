Oriental Beech Tree
The Oriental Beech Tree (Fagus orientalis) is a deciduous tree known for its tall, straight trunk and dense, spreading canopy. It is native to eastern Europe and western Asia and is commonly found in temperate forests.

Oriental Beech trees typically grow 30–40 meters tall with a broad crown. They prefer moist, well-drained soils and are often part of mixed hardwood forests. Their smooth gray bark and golden-green leaves make them visually striking throughout the growing season.

Characteristics of Oriental Beech Trees

  • Smooth, light gray bark
  • Dense, spreading canopy
  • Leaves are oval with wavy or serrated edges
  • Produces small triangular beechnuts
  • Fast-growing and long-lived
  • Native to eastern Europe and western Asia

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and veneers
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large gardens
  • Wildlife habitat and food source
  • Forest conservation and reforestation
  • Firewood and charcoal
