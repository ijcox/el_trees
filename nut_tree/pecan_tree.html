Pecan Tree
Pecan trees (Carya illinoinensis) are large deciduous trees native to North America, particularly the Mississippi River region. They are grown for their nutrient-rich nuts, which are widely used in culinary applications.

Pecan trees thrive in temperate climates with long growing seasons, full sunlight, and deep, well-drained soils. They are commonly planted in orchards and landscapes for nut production and shade.

Characteristics of Pecan Trees

  • Deciduous trees with tall, spreading crowns
  • Produce clusters of small greenish flowers in spring
  • Fruit is a hard-shelled nut with a rich, buttery kernel
  • Typically grow 70–100 feet tall under ideal conditions
  • Long-lived trees, often productive for over a century

Common Uses

  • Consumption of raw, roasted, or processed pecans
  • Used in baking, cooking, desserts, and confections
  • Commercial orchard farming for nut production
  • Shade tree for landscapes and parks
  • Wildlife food source for squirrels, birds, and other animals
