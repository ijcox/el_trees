European Chestnut Tree
The European Chestnut Tree (Castanea sativa) is native to southern Europe and Asia Minor. It is celebrated for its edible nuts, hardwood timber, and long lifespan.

European Chestnut trees grow 20–35 meters tall with a broad, rounded crown. They thrive in well-drained, acidic to neutral soils and full sunlight. Leaves are elongated, simple, and serrated. The tree produces spiny husks containing edible chestnuts.

Characteristics of European Chestnut Trees

  • Elongated, simple, serrated leaves
  • Broad, rounded crown
  • Long-lived, medium to large size
  • Produces edible nuts in spiny husks
  • Thrives in well-drained soils and full sun
  • Native to southern Europe and Asia Minor

Common Uses

  • Timber for furniture, flooring, and construction
  • Edible nuts for culinary use
  • Ornamental planting and shade tree
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for mammals and birds
  • Soil stabilization on slopes and hillsides
