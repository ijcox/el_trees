Scots Pine Tree
The Scots Pine Tree (Pinus sylvestris) is a hardy evergreen conifer native to Europe and Asia. It is known for its tall, straight trunk, distinctive orange-brown bark, and blue-green needles.

Scots Pine Trees can live up to 300 years and typically grow 35–45 meters tall. They are adapted to cold climates and poor soils, making them common in boreal forests and reforestation projects. Their cones provide seeds for wildlife, and the trees are valued for timber, resin, and ornamental planting.

Characteristics of Scots Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in pairs (4–7 cm long)
  • Bark is orange-brown and flaky on the upper trunk, thick and rough near the base
  • Produce oval cones (3–7 cm long) that release seeds for wind dispersal
  • Adapted to cold climates and poor, sandy or loamy soils
  • Lifespan ranges from 150 to 300 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction and furniture
  • Pulp for paper production
  • Resin for turpentine and rosin
  • Ornamental planting and landscaping
  • Reforestation and soil stabilization
