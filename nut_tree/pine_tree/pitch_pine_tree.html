Pitch Pine Tree
The Pitch Pine (Pinus rigida) is a hardy pine native to eastern North America. It is known for its thick, resinous bark and adaptability to poor, sandy, and fire-prone soils.

Pitch Pines typically grow 15–30 meters tall with irregular, open crowns. They have needle bundles of three, often twisted, and produce medium-sized cones. This species is fire-adapted, with cones that may open after exposure to heat, and is important for timber, resin, and wildlife habitat.

Characteristics of Pitch Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in bundles of three (7–12 cm long)
  • Bark is thick, dark brown, and resinous
  • Produce cones 4–7 cm long, often serotinous (open after fire)
  • Adapted to sandy, rocky, or poor soils and fire-prone areas
  • Lifespan typically 150–300 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, utility poles, and railroad ties
  • Resin production for turpentine
  • Reforestation and erosion control
  • Wildlife habitat for birds, squirrels, and small mammals
  • Ornamental planting in suitable landscapes
