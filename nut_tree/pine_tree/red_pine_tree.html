Red Pine Tree
The Red Pine (Pinus resinosa), also known as Norway Pine, is a tall evergreen native to northeastern North America. It is recognized for its reddish-brown bark, straight trunk, and adaptability to sandy, well-drained soils.

Red Pines typically grow 20–35 meters tall with a straight, cylindrical trunk. They have needles in bundles of two, and produce medium-sized cones. This species is commonly used in timber production, reforestation, and as an ornamental tree.

Characteristics of Red Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in bundles of two (12–18 cm long)
  • Bark is reddish-brown, thick, and scaly
  • Produce cones 4–7 cm long with winged seeds
  • Adapted to sandy, acidic soils and cold climates
  • Lifespan typically 200–300 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, utility poles, and furniture
  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Reforestation and erosion control
  • Ornamental planting in parks and large landscapes
  • Wildlife habitat for birds and small mammals
