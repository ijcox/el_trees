Loblolly Pine Tree
The Loblolly Pine (Pinus taeda) is a fast-growing pine native to the southeastern United States. It is widely cultivated for timber, pulpwood, and reforestation due to its adaptability and rapid growth.

Loblolly Pines can grow 25–35 meters tall and thrive in a variety of soils, particularly acidic, clay, or sandy soils. They have long needles in bundles of three and produce medium-sized cones. This species is important for timber production, ecosystem restoration, and wildlife habitat.

Characteristics of Loblolly Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in bundles of three (15–25 cm long)
  • Bark is reddish-brown and deeply furrowed
  • Produce cones 7–13 cm long with winged seeds
  • Fast-growing and tolerant of wet, clayey, or sandy soils
  • Lifespan typically 100–200 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for construction, furniture, and flooring
  • Pulpwood for paper production
  • Reforestation and ecosystem restoration
  • Windbreaks and erosion control
  • Wildlife habitat and food source for birds and small mammals
