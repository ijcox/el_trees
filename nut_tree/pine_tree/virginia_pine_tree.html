Virginia Pine Tree
The Virginia Pine (Pinus virginiana) is a hardy, fast-growing pine native to the eastern United States. It is often used in reforestation, timber production, and as a landscape tree for poor soils.

Virginia Pines typically grow 15–25 meters tall with a short, often crooked trunk and irregular crown. They have needles in bundles of two, and produce small to medium cones. This species is well-adapted to dry, sandy, or rocky soils and provides important habitat for wildlife.

Characteristics of Virginia Pine Trees

  • Evergreen conifers with needle-like leaves in bundles of two (7–12 cm long)
  • Bark is thin, scaly, and reddish-brown
  • Produce cones 4–7 cm long with winged seeds
  • Adapted to dry, sandy, or rocky soils and poor growing conditions
  • Lifespan typically 50–100 years

Common Uses

  • Timber for pulpwood, utility poles, and construction
  • Reforestation and erosion control on poor soils
  • Ornamental planting in landscapes and parks
  • Wildlife habitat for birds, squirrels, and small mammals
  • Windbreaks and soil stabilization
