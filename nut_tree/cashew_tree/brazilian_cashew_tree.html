Brazilian Cashew Tree
The Brazilian Cashew Tree (Anacardium occidentale, Brazilian cultivars) is a tropical variety selected for large fruit production and strong adaptation to coastal climates.

Brazilian cashew trees grow 8–14 meters tall with broad, spreading evergreen canopies. They thrive in hot coastal and tropical environments with sandy, well-drained soils. They are widely cultivated for both nut production and the large, juicy cashew apple.

Characteristics of Brazilian Cashew Trees

  • Evergreen tropical fruit tree
  • High fruit and nut yield cultivars
  • Adapted to coastal climates
  • Large cashew apples
  • Drought tolerant once established
  • Commercial plantation variety

Common Uses

  • Cashew nut production
  • Juice and beverage production
  • Food processing industry
  • Export agriculture
  • Local fruit consumption
