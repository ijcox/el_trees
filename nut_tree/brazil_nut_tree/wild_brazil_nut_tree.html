Wild Brazil Nut Tree
The Wild Brazil Nut Tree (Bertholletia excelsa) is a massive Amazon rainforest tree known for producing large woody fruit pods containing edible Brazil nuts.

Wild Brazil nut trees grow 30–50 meters tall and are found deep in the Amazon rainforest. They depend heavily on intact forest ecosystems and natural pollinators such as large-bodied bees. Their seed pods fall from great heights and are harvested from the forest floor.

Characteristics of Wild Brazil Nut Trees

  • Very tall emergent rainforest tree
  • Produces hard woody seed pods
  • Wild-harvested, not easily domesticated
  • Requires intact rainforest ecosystems
  • Long-lived (often centuries)
  • Ecologically important species

Common Uses

  • Wild Brazil nut harvesting
  • Food export industry
  • Rainforest conservation value
  • Natural forest economies
  • Ecotourism and sustainable trade
