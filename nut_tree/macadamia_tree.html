Macadamia Nut Tree
Macadamia trees (Macadamia integrifolia and related species) are evergreen nut trees native to eastern Australia. They are cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide for their rich, buttery nuts.

Macadamia trees grow best in warm, frost-free climates with well-drained soil and full sunlight. They are commonly planted in orchards for commercial nut production and occasionally as ornamental trees.

Characteristics of Macadamia Trees

  • Evergreen trees with glossy, lance-shaped leaves
  • Typically grow 20–40 feet tall, occasionally taller in ideal conditions
  • Produce small, fragrant flowers in clusters
  • Nuts are hard-shelled with rich, creamy kernels
  • Require warm temperatures, well-drained soil, and moderate irrigation

Common Uses

  • Consumption of raw, roasted, or processed macadamia nuts
  • Used in confectionery, baking, and snacks
  • Extraction of macadamia oil for cooking and cosmetic use
  • Ornamental planting in tropical/subtropical gardens
  • Commercial orchard farming for nut production
