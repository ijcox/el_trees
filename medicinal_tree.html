Medicinal Trees
Medicinal trees are species whose bark, leaves, fruits, or other parts are used in traditional and modern medicine for their therapeutic properties. They have been valued for centuries for their health benefits, including anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and antioxidant effects.

Medicinal trees grow in a variety of climates, from tropical to temperate regions. They are often cultivated in herbal gardens, plantations, and natural forests for use in remedies, extracts, and pharmaceutical products.

Characteristics of Medicinal Trees

  • Often deciduous or evergreen, depending on species
  • Leaves, bark, fruits, seeds, or resin are harvested for medicinal use
  • Some have strong aromatic or bitter compounds indicating therapeutic properties
  • Moderate to fast growth rate depending on species
  • Adapted to specific climates and soil types

Common Uses

  • Herbal remedies for inflammation, fever, and infections
  • Pharmaceutical extracts such as aspirin (from willow bark)
  • Essential oils and aromatherapy products
  • Traditional medicine in cultural and indigenous practices
  • Nutritional supplements from fruits, seeds, and leaves
  • Planting in medicinal or botanical gardens for educational purposes
