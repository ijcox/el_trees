Deciduous Tree
Deciduous trees are trees that shed their leaves seasonally, usually in the autumn. This adaptation helps them conserve water and survive colder or drier seasons.

Most deciduous trees grow in temperate climates where seasonal changes are significant. During the growing season they produce broad leaves for photosynthesis, and before winter these leaves change color and fall off.

Characteristics of Deciduous Trees

  • Lose their leaves seasonally, usually in autumn
  • Typically have broad, flat leaves
  • Grow primarily in temperate climates
  • Often produce flowers, fruits, or nuts
  • Many species display seasonal color changes

Common Uses

  • Timber and hardwood lumber
  • Furniture and cabinetry
  • Shade trees in urban and suburban areas
  • Food production such as fruits and nuts
  • Landscaping and ornamental planting
