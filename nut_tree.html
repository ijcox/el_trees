Nut Tree
Nut trees are trees that produce edible seeds encased in hard shells, commonly known as nuts. These trees are cultivated for food, oil, and sometimes timber.

Nut trees grow in a variety of climates, including temperate and subtropical regions. They are often deciduous and provide both ecological and economic benefits.

Common Types of Nut Trees

Almond Tree Walnut Tree Hazelnut Tree Pecan Tree Cashew Tree Macadamia Tree Brazil Nut Tree Chestnut Tree Coconut Tree Pine Tree (Pine Nuts) Beech Tree (Beechnuts)

Characteristics of Nut Trees

  • Produce edible seeds encased in hard shells
  • Often deciduous
  • Grow in temperate or subtropical climates
  • Require pollination for nut production
  • Provide food for humans, wildlife, and livestock

Common Uses

  • Fresh or roasted nut consumption
  • Nut oils for cooking and cosmetics
  • Commercial agriculture and orchards
  • Timber for woodworking in some species
  • Wildlife habitat and ecological contributions
